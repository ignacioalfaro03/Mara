# P0 Unexpected Attraction + Voice Intensity Test Plan

## Purpose

Validate two product hypotheses without production adult media, persistent adult-sensitive memory or paid voice infrastructure:

1. users value Mara noticing an unexpected attraction/reaction;
2. seduction is stronger when Mara has vocal contrast instead of sounding maximally sexual all the time.

This is a manual/DEV research plan only.

## Hypothesis A — Unexpected Attraction

The strongest moment may not be `Mara showed exactly what I already knew I liked`.

It may be:

> `Mara showed me something adjacent/unexpected, noticed my reaction and understood what part actually worked.`

Test sequence:

1. show an abstract adult-safe candidate description or approved test stimulus;
2. ask the tester to choose expected reaction before seeing/hearing it;
3. after the stimulus, capture `positive`, `mixed`, `negative`, `surprised me`;
4. if `surprised me`, ask one bounded follow-up about the dimension that worked;
5. Mara gives a short playful interpretation;
6. tester marks `felt perceptive` or `wrong / too much`.

Do not collect real sexual history or infer sexual orientation.

## Trans-inclusive research boundary

A test stimulus may conceptually include an adult trans performer only where lawful/consensual and tester-appropriate.

The research question is never:

> `Does this prove the tester's orientation?`

It is:

> `Which content dimensions generated the reaction?`

Do not frame trans identity itself as shameful or inherently taboo.

## Hypothesis B — Voice Intensity Contrast

Test the same canonical Mara line/scene with three performance directions:

- V1 — flirty;
- V2 — seductive;
- V3 — high-intensity intimate performance.

Do not use V3 repeatedly in the same session.

Ask:

- Which sounded most like Mara?
- Which was most attractive?
- Which felt overacted?
- Would V3 still feel special if heard every session?
- Did V1/V2 make V3 more effective through contrast?

## Hypothesis C — Teasing Observation

Compare:

A. long explanation of what Mara thinks the user liked;
B. one short confident observation + correction option.

Expected winner is B if Mara is perceptive enough.

The goal is `Mara noticed`, not `Mara diagnosed me`.

## Hypothesis D — Correction

A misread must be cheap to correct.

Tester should be able to respond conceptually:

- `yes, that's it`;
- `not exactly`;
- `no`;
- `don't use that again`.

Wrong inference should reduce confidence rather than become a durable label.

Do not log raw adult wording, performer identity category, anatomical reaction text or inferred orientation to generic analytics.

## Anti-fatigue test

Specifically test whether high-intensity voice degrades after repetition.

Suggested manual comparison:

- Group/sequence A: V3 once after V1/V2 buildup.
- Group/sequence B: V3 repeatedly.

Measure qualitative attraction, authenticity and annoyance/overacting.

Do not conclude from one small sample; this is directional P0 evidence.

## Decision rule

Advance only if:

- users describe Mara as more perceptive, not creepy;
- surprise creates curiosity rather than pressure;
- correction feels safe;
- voice contrast materially improves attraction;
- V3 is clearly more valuable when rare;
- Mara still sounds like one coherent character.

Do not advance if:

- surprise depends on shame;
- testers feel categorized or diagnosed;
- high-intensity voice becomes porn-TTS parody;
- the mechanism creates pressure to disclose identity/orientation;
- the signal is used to alter price or spending pressure.

## P0 boundary

No:

- production adult media integration;
- trans-category targeting in public analytics;
- sexual-orientation inference;
- paid voice provider;
- production V3 voice automation;
- persistent adult-sensitive profile;
- real payment changes;
- deploy.
