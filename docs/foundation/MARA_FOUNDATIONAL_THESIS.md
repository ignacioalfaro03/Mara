# MARA — FOUNDATIONAL THESIS

Status: **SOLE HIGHEST-PRIORITY PRODUCT AND COMPANY AUTHORITY**  
Effective: **2026-09-12**

## Founder decision

The company and product are called **Mara**.

Mara is no longer defined as Mara Vera, a virtual character, chatbot, AI companion, immersive world, adult character product, or consumer social app.

Those older concepts are historical product experiments only. They do not define the company and must not control roadmap, architecture, navigation, metrics, positioning, or launch sequencing.

If an older document conflicts with this thesis, **this thesis wins**.

## Company thesis

# MARA IS THE REVENUE OS FOR CREATORS.

Mara helps creators turn an audience they already have into customers, repeat customers, and measurable revenue.

Core loop:

`AUDIENCE → CUSTOMER → COMMERCIAL CONTEXT → OFFER → PURCHASE → LEARNING → NEXT BEST ACTION → REPURCHASE`

The creator brings demand. Mara helps monetize that demand better.

## Core problem

Creators can usually see followers, views, likes and comments, but often cannot answer commercially important questions:

- who buys;
- what each customer buys;
- how much each customer spends;
- who is likely to buy again;
- who is becoming inactive;
- what offer should be sent next;
- when to contact a customer;
- which products create repeat purchase;
- which customers are high value;
- where revenue is being left on the table.

Mara exists to close that gap.

## Product definition

Mara combines:

- creator commerce;
- checkout and payments;
- creator-scoped CRM;
- customer history;
- offers and products;
- analytics;
- lifecycle segmentation;
- automations;
- opportunity detection;
- Next Best Action;
- Next Best Offer;
- revenue attribution;
- AI as a commercial decision layer.

AI is not the product. **Creator revenue lift is the product outcome.**

## Product surfaces

### Creator side — primary product

The creator surface is the operating system of the business.

It must answer quickly:

1. What happened?
2. Who needs attention?
3. What should I sell now?
4. What is the highest-value action I can take next?
5. Is Mara increasing my revenue?

Primary creator surfaces:

- Home / Today;
- Opportunities;
- Customers / CRM;
- Offers / Products;
- Orders / Fulfillment;
- Automations;
- Analytics;
- Payments / Payouts when real payment rails are authorized.

### Fan side — mobile web first

Fans should not be forced to install an app before buying.

Initial consumer path:

`SOCIAL / LINK / QR → CREATOR STOREFRONT → OFFER → CHECKOUT → PURCHASE → CONFIRMATION / DELIVERY`

The fan experience must be fast, trustworthy and mobile-first.

A standalone consumer app is **not an MVP dependency**. Build it only after evidence of high repeat usage, multi-creator behavior and network-level retention.

## North Star

# GMV PER ACTIVE CREATOR

Supporting metrics:

- visitor → buyer conversion;
- average order value;
- repeat purchase rate;
- returning-buyer GMV;
- revenue per buyer;
- creator activation rate;
- time to first sale;
- D30/D90 creator retention;
- GMV retention;
- creator net earnings;
- Mara take rate;
- contribution margin.

Do not optimize primarily for messages, session length, lore engagement, AI usage, or social-feed activity.

## Strategic metric

Mara must eventually measure:

# MARA REVENUE LIFT

This must be evidence-based, not marketing fiction.

Use cohorts, holdouts, A/B tests and attribution to estimate incremental revenue generated by Mara recommendations, automations and lifecycle actions.

## Canonical vertical slice

The immediate product loop is:

`CREATOR → OFFER → FAN → CHECKOUT → PAYMENT → CUSTOMER → CRM → OPPORTUNITY → SECOND PURCHASE`

Everything that does not help close or improve this loop is lower priority.

## First magic moments

Creator Magic Moment #1:

> Someone bought from my link and is now a customer I can understand and serve better.

Creator Magic Moment #2:

> Mara identified an opportunity, I acted on it, and it generated another purchase.

The second moment is the differentiation target.

## Opportunity Engine

The Creator Home should prioritize actions over charts.

Examples:

- recover abandoned checkouts;
- contact repeat-ready buyers;
- reactivate high-value inactive customers;
- promote a product that is outperforming;
- create a bundle from observed purchase patterns;
- fulfill paid orders;
- avoid contacting a customer when no action is warranted.

Principle:

# COMPLEX INTELLIGENCE UNDERNEATH. OBVIOUS ACTION ABOVE.

## Customer relationship as the unit of value

The central asset is not a message, follower or content item.

It is the creator-scoped commercial relationship:

`CREATOR ↔ CUSTOMER`

Mara should make that relationship more understandable, easier to operate and more valuable over time.

## Data / intelligence direction

Potential long-term moat:

`TRANSACTIONS → CUSTOMER HISTORY → BEHAVIOR → PATTERNS → RECOMMENDATIONS → BETTER CONVERSION → MORE TRANSACTIONS`

Do not claim a moat before the loop has real volume and measurable performance.

Never build a hidden vulnerability dossier. Data must be creator-scoped, purpose-limited, privacy-aware and governed by consent / applicable law.

## Business model hypothesis

Default launch hypothesis:

- low or zero fixed entry cost;
- success-based platform take rate;
- optional Creator Pro later for advanced CRM, automation, intelligence and team features.

Indicative take-rate range for validation: **10–15%**, not a permanent pricing commitment.

Pricing must be tested against payment costs, support, fraud, refunds, compliance, creator economics and willingness to pay.

## Initial ICP

Start narrow.

Prioritize creators who:

- already have an audience;
- can sell something legal and policy-compliant;
- rely heavily on social platforms;
- have limited CRM sophistication;
- want more predictable monetization;
- have enough audience engagement to generate early transaction signal.

A working hypothesis is approximately 5K–250K followers, but actual revenue potential matters more than follower count.

## What not to build now

Until evidence changes the decision, do not prioritize:

- Mara Vera as a character product;
- AI girlfriend / companion;
- chatbot-first UX;
- virtual worlds / lore;
- consumer social feed;
- generic discovery marketplace;
- native fan app;
- metaverse / 3D;
- gamification systems;
- crypto / tokens;
- complex IRL marketplace;
- new experimental labs disconnected from revenue validation.

Historical code may remain temporarily if removing it creates unnecessary risk, but it must not be treated as canonical product direction.

## Reuse rule

Do not rebuild working infrastructure.

Preserve and adapt where useful:

- auth/session;
- creator identity;
- creator storefront/profile;
- offers;
- checkout intents;
- purchases;
- entitlements / fulfillment;
- creator-customer relationship model;
- CRM summaries;
- Next Best Action;
- telemetry;
- RLS / tenant isolation;
- CI/CD and hosted proof infrastructure.

## Payments and ledger

Never infer money from UI state.

Canonical financial primitives should include:

- Order;
- Payment;
- Refund;
- Processor Fee;
- Platform Fee;
- Creator Earning;
- Payout;
- Balance Transaction.

Every monetary movement must be traceable and idempotent.

Real payment and payout activation remains gated by provider eligibility, compliance and explicit founder authorization.

## Product build filter

Every feature must materially support at least one:

1. increase conversion;
2. increase repeat purchase;
3. increase AOV;
4. reduce creator workload;
5. reduce creator/customer churn;
6. improve trust;
7. improve commercial intelligence.

If not, defer it.

## MVP definition

The MVP is not “a complete creator platform”.

It is proof that:

1. a creator can activate;
2. create a public storefront/profile;
3. create an offer;
4. share a link;
5. a fan can buy;
6. the order is recorded;
7. the fan becomes a creator-scoped customer;
8. the creator sees the customer and revenue;
9. Mara can produce at least one useful commercial opportunity/action.

## Validation sequence

Validate in order:

- H1: creators want to publish offers through Mara;
- H2: fans buy with low friction;
- H3: creators return to operate their business;
- H4: CRM context changes creator behavior;
- H5: Opportunity Engine recommendations are used;
- H6: those actions create incremental revenue;
- H7: creators accept Mara's economics.

## Initial evidence gates

First practical targets:

- 5 activated creators;
- 5 published offers;
- 10 real transactions;
- at least one repeat buyer;
- first real platform revenue;
- first creator with measurable Mara Revenue Lift.

Then scale to:

- 20 creators;
- 100 buyers;
- meaningful recurring GMV;
- repeat behavior and retention.
