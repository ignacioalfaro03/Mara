# Mara Vera — Traction → Investment Gate

## Purpose

Prevent premature SaaS, API and infrastructure spend while preserving the ability to invest quickly once Mara demonstrates real momentum.

Default launch rule:

> **Direct recurring OPEX should remain approximately $0 until there is evidence that Mara is gaining force and a specific paid capability has a measurable business case.**

This is not an anti-investment rule. It is a sequencing rule.

## What counts as traction

Traction is evidence that real users are moving deeper into Mara's funnel, not merely that the team can generate attractive content.

Signals may include:

### Distribution

- sustained organic view growth;
- increasing reach from non-followers;
- repeat high-performing posts rather than one isolated viral spike;
- growing follower base with acceptable follow/view conversion.

### Engagement quality

- shares;
- saves;
- meaningful comments;
- DMs;
- people asking for more or returning to ongoing threads;
- profile visits increasing with content reach.

### First-party intent

- social → web clicks;
- returning web visitors;
- account/waitlist registrations when available;
- users initiating conversations;
- repeated sessions.

### Commercial intent

- explicit willingness to pay;
- premium CTA clicks;
- purchases;
- subscriptions;
- paid personalized requests;
- second purchases / repeat spend.

Revenue is the strongest signal, but the system must also recognize strong pre-revenue demand when monetization has not yet been activated.

## The First-Dollar Gate

A paid tool or API should be approved only when all three conditions are satisfied:

### A. Traction evidence exists

At least one meaningful acquisition/engagement/first-party/commercial signal is sustained or repeated enough that it is unlikely to be a single random event.

### B. A measured bottleneck exists

Examples:

- free generation capacity is preventing the team from publishing a format that is already working;
- free output repeatedly fails quality while a benchmarked paid provider materially improves Approval Yield;
- TPAA is high enough that a paid tool can clearly remove a recurring production bottleneck;
- a voice/realtime capability is required by real returning users;
- a paid plan is required for commercial rights on content already proven to perform.

"It looks better" or "other creators use it" is not a sufficient bottleneck.

### C. A business case exists

Before spending, document:

- tool/provider;
- monthly or variable cost;
- exact problem solved;
- KPI expected to improve;
- expected time/output improvement;
- commercial-rights requirement if relevant;
- cheapest viable alternative;
- cancellation/rollback path.

If the expected benefit cannot be stated, do not buy.

## Gate states

### RED — no spend

Use when:

- distribution has no convincing signal;
- engagement is shallow or random;
- no first-party demand exists;
- no monetization evidence exists;
- or there is no measured tooling bottleneck.

Action:

**keep OPEX ≈ $0, continue manual/free experimentation, or change the content/positioning hypothesis.**

### YELLOW — bounded experiments only

Use when attention is emerging but the business loop is not yet proven.

Allowed only with explicit founder authorization:

- one-off or tightly capped usage test;
- short trial that cannot unexpectedly auto-renew;
- very small API budget with a hard cap;
- commercial-license upgrade required to publish a proven asset format.

Do not convert YELLOW into multiple concurrent subscriptions.

### GREEN — invest against a proven bottleneck

Use when Mara has demonstrated meaningful repeated demand and the paid capability has a documented expected return.

GREEN does not mean "buy the full stack".

It means approve the smallest plan/capacity necessary to solve the proven bottleneck, then remeasure.

## Decision priority

When a bottleneck appears, solve it in this order:

1. better process;
2. reuse/edit approved assets;
3. free allowance or credits;
4. lower-cost provider;
5. bounded paid test;
6. recurring subscription;
7. automation/infrastructure scale.

Skip directly to a higher step only when evidence justifies it.

## Metrics to review before spend

Use the metrics already available for the current phase. Relevant examples:

- follower growth;
- follow/view conversion;
- shares/view;
- saves/view;
- profile visits/view;
- web CTR;
- returning visitors;
- conversation starts;
- repeat users;
- premium CTA rate;
- payer conversion;
- repeat-purchase rate;
- ARPPU;
- CPAA;
- TPAA;
- Approval Yield.

Do not invent precision where sample sizes are small. Directional repeated evidence is more useful than fake statistical confidence.

## Subscription hygiene

For every paid subscription eventually approved:

- owner must be known;
- renewal date must be recorded;
- cancellation path must be known;
- purpose/KPI must be recorded;
- usage must be reviewed before renewal;
- duplicate providers should be removed when a winner is established.

A subscription that no longer has an active business case should be cancelled.

## Relationship Engine rule

Realtime voice, persistent memory, complex personalization and proprietary relationship infrastructure remain post-validation investments.

They can be prototyped with free/bounded resources, but recurring production spend should follow evidence of returning users and relationship demand.

## Founder decision rule

The burden of proof is on the expense.

The question is not:

> "Can this tool make Mara better?"

Almost every premium tool can.

The question is:

> **"Has Mara already earned the right to spend this money, and can we measure what this expense should improve?"**
