# Mara Vera — Monetization Engine

## Status

Authoritative commercial architecture for the Foundation outcome. It extends `monetization.md` with offer design, sequencing, experimentation and unit economics. It does not authorize real payments or providers.

## Objective

Turn desire and personal relevance into **repeatable, transparent, high-margin revenue** without making spending a proxy for affection or relationship closeness.

Commercial priority:

**first purchase → value confirmation → second purchase → repeat spend → retained relationship**.

The [Desire Discovery Engine](../architecture/desire-discovery-engine.md), [Preference Graph](../architecture/preference-graph.md), [Fantasy Compiler](../architecture/fantasy-compiler.md) and [Momentum Commerce](../architecture/momentum-commerce.md) layer may improve **offer relevance, experience composition, timing and post-purchase continuity**. They must never become vulnerability scoring or opaque individualized pricing infrastructure.

## Monetization ladder

### Layer 0 — Free attention
Purpose: acquisition.

Surfaces:
- social content;
- lifestyle/editorial imagery;
- platform-safe teasing;
- short voice/video clips;
- humor/opinions;
- mini stories/open loops;
- public personality;
- platform-safe polls/A-B choices.

The free layer creates curiosity about Mara, not complete premium fulfillment.

### Layer 1 — Free relationship + playable discovery
Purpose: move from follower to identified/returning user while making personalization enjoyable.

Candidate experiences:
- limited conversation;
- 3-choice playable onboarding;
- Fast Five / Mara Test;
- preference discovery;
- Mara predictions;
- correction/reveal loops;
- lightweight callbacks;
- previews/story choices;
- first voice exposure;
- adult-mode discovery without automatic escalation.

P0 can remain manual/rule-based.

### Layer 2 — Low-ticket first purchase
Purpose: lowest-friction proof of willingness to pay.

Candidates:
- special audio;
- mini story;
- themed unlock;
- continuation;
- personalized short message;
- visual pack;
- bounded mini experience;
- simple interactive branch;
- compiled mini experience.

Measure visitor→registration, registration→first paid, time to first purchase, first ticket, conversion, refund/dispute and preference-relevant vs generic performance.

### Layer 3 — Repeat purchases
Purpose: prove delivered value.

Candidates:
- sequels;
- episodic stories;
- additional voice moments;
- themed drops;
- follow-up personalization;
- branching continuations;
- bundles;
- co-created `Build It` experiences;
- compiled variations that meaningfully differ.

Commercial proof point: **first payer → second payer**.

### Layer 4 — Membership
Do not assume subscription is default.

Membership becomes attractive only after repeat usage proves recurring value.

Potential benefits:
- recurring drops;
- selected premium content;
- deeper interaction allowance;
- additional voice;
- continuity conveniences;
- earlier access;
- selected personalization;
- member-only arcs.

Membership is a potential **base premium relationship layer**, not necessarily the revenue ceiling. Clearly scoped PPV moments, continuations, customs, Build It, limited drops and future live/realtime products may sit above membership.

### Layer 5 — Custom / premium
Higher-value experiences may include:
- personalized voice;
- longer narratives;
- multi-format bundles;
- bounded configurable themes;
- scoped roleplay;
- priority/limited scope;
- co-created story direction.

High ticket comes from scope, personalization and perceived value, not financial/emotional pressure.

## Fantasy Compiler commercial role

The Fantasy Compiler changes the business from selling categories to selling **relevant combinations**.

Instead of:

> Dominance Pack / Roleplay Pack / Audio Pack

Mara may compose:

> selective energy + teasing + voice + work context + continuation + medium novelty

and route the user to a suitable free or paid experience.

This is commercially useful because it can improve:
- perceived relevance;
- discovery;
- conversion;
- continuation;
- repeat purchase;
- content reuse;
- defensibility.

It must not optimize for “maximum extractable spend”.

## Momentum Commerce

The commercial experience should optimize **where the user already wants more**, not generic paywall density.

Canonical loop:

**Presence → Interaction → Desire → Personal Relevance → High-Value Moment → Clear Optional Offer → Purchase → Immediate Gratification → Experience Continues → Ownership/Progress → Natural Next Opportunity**.

Permanent principle:

> **Payment must be a comma, not a period.**

Where technically possible, successful checkout returns to the exact branch/line/state that created the intent.

Do not route a user to Home/store after a contextual purchase unless the product itself requires it.

See [Momentum Commerce / Scarcity / Reward Architecture](../architecture/momentum-commerce.md).

## Commercial moment taxonomy

Potential moments:
- curiosity → mini unlock / continuation / voice reveal;
- personal relevance → contextual/personalized version;
- participation → Build It / additional control;
- anticipation → continuation / alternate path;
- achievement → deeper experience / collection progression;
- real scarcity → custom slot / dated drop / limited edition / live window.

A moment should only become commercial when the user understands the value before purchase and declining does not damage baseline relationship treatment.

## Discovery-informed offer relevance

Preference signals can change:
- recommendation order;
- highlighted experience;
- modality;
- story family;
- continuation priority;
- known-fit vs exploration mode;
- reward style where contextual and consent-compatible.

Examples:
- reliable voice affinity → feature voice experience;
- narrative affinity → feature continuation;
- personalization affinity → custom experience;
- exploration preference → `Surprise Me`;
- repeated co-creation → `Build It`;
- repeated praise preference → allow occasional earned praise within compatible experiences.

Do not use Preference Graph/Fantasy Compiler to:
- estimate maximum price tolerance;
- target financial distress/debt;
- exploit loneliness/dependency;
- hide cheaper equivalents based on vulnerability;
- alter emotional treatment based on expected spend.

Pricing remains transparent and SKU/cohort-based.

## Personalized storefront

Future sections:
- `For You`;
- `Mara's Pick`;
- `Continue`;
- `Surprise Me`;
- `Build It`;
- `This Week` / `Limited` only when availability is real;
- `New`;
- `Trending` only with real data.

A future storefront may rank differently using explicit/high-confidence preference data, purchased entitlements, story history, format affinity, current context, relationship continuity and exploration mode.

Same SKU retains the same applicable price/terms under current pricing rules.

## Experience Configurator / Build It

Do not treat custom requests as an unstructured inbox forever.

Bounded variables may include:
- tone;
- theme/scenario;
- outfit/content direction;
- preferred name;
- duration;
- Mara intensity band;
- format;
- voice;
- narrative style;
- ending/continuation;
- personalization depth.

`Build It` can become the user-facing composition surface.

Pricing may eventually depend on real cost/scope drivers such as duration, modalities, personalization depth, exclusivity and operational priority.

Strategic target:

**mass personalization with premium perceived value**.

## Pay for agency

A scalable value ladder can charge for **meaningful influence**, not merely more explicitness.

Potential progression:
- free: Mara curates most variables;
- paid: user controls one meaningful branch/variable;
- premium: more variables/modalities;
- custom: deeper bounded co-creation.

Agency must visibly change the outcome.

## Pay for personalization

Personalization depth can be a product/cost scope driver:
- P0 generic but Mara-specific;
- P1 nominal/contextual;
- P2 preference-aware;
- P3 continuity-aware;
- P4 relationship-aware after persistent memory is justified.

Higher price only makes sense if users value the difference and contribution margin remains healthy.

## Real scarcity, drops and collectibility

Supported only when a real enforceable constraint exists:
- real limited drops;
- dated releases;
- real custom capacity;
- fixed editions;
- narrative windows tied to actual Mara Life State;
- future live windows with real concurrency/cost constraints;
- legitimate early access;
- episodic collections;
- user-visible acquired history.

Permanent rule:

> **Every scarcity claim must map to an enforceable product constraint.**

Prohibited:
- fake countdowns;
- reset-on-refresh timers;
- fake inventory;
- fake viewers/buyers;
- fabricated urgency;
- fake competing suitors;
- “Mara chose someone else” pressure.

Collectibility supports continuity/ownership, not emotional debt.

## Capacity scarcity

Launch custom experiences can truthfully be capacity-limited when manual generation, voice, QC, review or revisions impose a real throughput ceiling.

Track per slot:
- price;
- fulfillment time;
- generation/review cost;
- revisions;
- contribution margin;
- satisfaction;
- repeat purchase.

Real slot limits can protect quality/margin and reveal demand before automation.

## Ownership vs access

Separate:

### Access
Temporary entitlement under membership/window terms.

### Ownership
User keeps the acquired digital experience/entitlement under stated terms.

Do not market access as ownership.

`My History with Mara` can contain appropriate acquired episodes, branches, voice/media, limited drops, custom experiences, collection progress and future continuation eligibility.

## Collections and completion

Collections may group explicit items such as a Night Series.

Progress can be visible (`3 / 4`) without purchase pressure.

A completion reward/epilogue should be defined clearly enough before purchases that the bargain is not changed retroactively.

## Continuation / Commercial Graph

Each eligible experience may define zero or more next commercial edges:
- continuation;
- alternate branch;
- personalized version;
- voice upgrade;
- Build It;
- custom.

The purpose is to intentionally test the strongest **first purchase → second purchase** path, not to force an upsell after every purchase.

No fake cliffhangers.

## Reward Grammar

Post-purchase/experience reward styles may include:
- praise;
- teasing;
- acknowledgement;
- challenge completion;
- reveal;
- surprise;
- access;
- progression;
- collectible;
- none.

Contextual praise such as “Good boy”, “Así me gusta” or “Te lo ganaste” can be powerful when it fits a consented interaction dynamic.

Permanent principle:

> **Reward should feel earned, not mechanically purchased.**

Do not implement `payment → affection`.

A paid experience can contain a reward because of the completed interaction/experience. Payment itself never raises relationship stage, baseline affection, respect or Mara's emotional stability.

Use cooldown/recent-use logic so rare reactions retain meaning.

## Relational friction vs commerce

Mara can be warm, playful, distracted, annoyed, distant, reflective or repaired based on Life State, conversation, boundaries and real interaction history.

She may answer later, disagree, refuse, become shorter or repair a disagreement.

Commercial state must not be the cause of relational punishment.

Never:
- withdraw affection because an offer was declined;
- sell forgiveness;
- punish failed payment;
- manufacture abandonment/jealousy to increase spend.

## High-value users

Commercial state may identify `high_value` for service and offer relevance.

Allowed levers:
- bigger bundles;
- deeper personalization;
- priority fulfillment where sold;
- exclusive scope;
- early access;
- longer custom experiences;
- optional collection/status mechanics.

Never use debt, distress, loneliness, bereavement, inferred dependency, compulsive-spending signals, threats or withdrawal of baseline respect.

**High ARPPU through value, not coercion.**

## Contextual microtransactions

> **Monetize moments of high perceived value, not every click.**

Each paid action must clearly state product, price, scope, recurring/nonrecurring status and next step.

A discovery/compiler result may lead to a relevant product, but the transition from play to commerce must be obvious.

Do not surprise-charge inside adult/aroused moments.

## Post-purchase experience

The first seconds after a real purchase are part of the product.

Target sequence:

**transaction success → entitlement → exact-state resume → Mara reaction → immediate payoff/clear fulfillment → continuation → ownership/progression write**.

Track:
- Purchase Resume Success;
- Post-Purchase Continuation Rate;
- Commercial Inertia;
- second purchase after successful payoff.

Do not use a generic receipt page as the emotional conclusion of the experience.

## Commercial Memory / Purchase Satisfaction

Keep commercial evidence separate from Relationship Memory.

Useful records:
- SKU/offer;
- entitlement/ownership;
- modality;
- completed/replayed;
- sequel/continuation request;
- refund/dispute;
- satisfaction;
- first→second linkage;
- collection progress.

A purchase alone is weak evidence. Sequences such as `bought voice → completed → replayed → bought continuation` are stronger product signals.

Commercial Memory can improve relevance. It cannot create relationship closeness or emotional pressure.

## Intent recovery

If a user has already expressed legitimate premium intent, Mara may later surface that unfinished value through continuity, e.g.:

> “Te dejé lo de ayer ahí.”

Do not fabricate “last chance” framing unless a real deadline exists.

## Pricing architecture

All numeric pricing is experimental until purchase data exists.

Test:
- product type;
- bundle composition;
- duration;
- personalization depth;
- voice inclusion;
- exclusivity;
- membership structure;
- real scarcity type where applicable.

Measure conversion, revenue, first→second purchase, AOV, ARPPU, repeat purchase, refund/dispute, churn after purchase, contribution margin and qualitative satisfaction.

Avoid opaque individualized pricing.

## Compiled experience economics

For every compiled paid experience:

**Contribution Margin = Revenue − variable generation − review/moderation − storage/delivery − provider/payment fees − support/refund cost**.

Also track:
- number of reused content blocks;
- incremental personalization cost;
- compile/fulfillment time;
- voice/video cost;
- approval/rework rate;
- conversion;
- continuation;
- second-purchase linkage;
- repeat-family/cross-family behavior;
- Personalization Lift;
- scarcity/collection operations cost where relevant.

Do not assume deeper personalization is automatically more profitable.

Prefer:

**high perceived value / low marginal cost / high repeatability / controlled risk**.

A key Fantasy Compiler hypothesis is **mass personalization without mass production**.

## Fantasy family performance

Do not rank families only by gross revenue.

For each family evaluate:
- demand;
- conversion;
- return;
- second purchase;
- retention;
- contribution margin;
- production complexity;
- support burden;
- compliance/platform/payment risk.

Best family = **best healthy economics**, not maximum explicitness.

## P0 commercial implementation

Before real payments, P0 may contain:
- 3–5 structured mock commercial moments;
- one clearly labeled prototype scarcity example;
- one collection/progress example;
- continuation graph metadata;
- reward-style metadata;
- premium-intent events;
- development-only mock purchase/resume and reward delivery.

Rules:
- premium intent is never presented as a purchase;
- fake/static capacity is visibly prototype/demo data;
- mock purchase is test/development only;
- no real payment/provider activation follows from these mocks.

## Commercial decision rule

Prioritize only features that plausibly improve **conversion, repeat spend, retention, margin or defensibility** without disproportionate legal, policy, privacy, trust or operating risk.

The strongest commercial experience should feel like **more Mara**, not more checkout.
