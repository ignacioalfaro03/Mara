# MARA — PILOT OPERATING SYSTEM V1

Status: operating control system  
Effective: 2026-09-13  
Parent: `MARA_FIRST_10_CREATORS_PLAYBOOK.md`  
Founder boundary: no merge or production activation without explicit founder authorization.

## Purpose

Turn Mara's first creator pilots into auditable commercial experiments.

The pilot is not a demo. The pilot is a controlled attempt to prove that Mara can identify a monetizable opportunity, get a creator to act on it, and generate measurable commercial value.

Canonical loop:

`CREATOR BASELINE → CUSTOMER SIGNAL → MARA OPPORTUNITY → CREATOR APPROVAL → ACTION → COMMERCIAL OUTCOME → EVIDENCE → PRODUCT DECISION`

## 1. Pilot stages

Every creator must occupy exactly one stage.

### P0 — PROSPECT
Research card complete. No outreach yet.

Exit gate:
- prospect score >= 9/12;
- real monetization exists;
- repeat/upsell potential exists;
- creator is realistically reachable.

### P1 — DISCOVERY
Creator has replied and discovery is scheduled or completed.

Exit gate:
- current offer understood;
- buyer journey understood;
- customer tracking method understood;
- measurable revenue problem identified;
- creator agrees that the problem is worth testing.

### P2 — BASELINE
Commercial baseline captured before Mara recommends anything.

Required minimum:
- active offer(s);
- price or ticket range;
- recent customer/sales period;
- known repeat buyers if available;
- existing follow-up method;
- baseline period start/end;
- known constraints on customer data and outreach.

No baseline = no credible incremental revenue claim.

### P3 — OPPORTUNITY READY
Mara has produced one or more evidence-backed actions.

Every opportunity must include:
- creator;
- customer or segment;
- signal/evidence;
- recommended action;
- proposed offer;
- why now;
- expected value hypothesis;
- confidence class;
- creator approval status.

Confidence classes:
- A = direct commercial signal;
- B = strong behavioral/relationship signal;
- C = plausible but weak evidence.

Early pilots should prioritize A and B.

### P4 — ACTIONED
Creator has executed at least one approved action.

Required evidence:
- action type;
- channel;
- timestamp;
- recipient/segment reference;
- actual message/offer used or evidence link;
- Mara recommendation ID/reference.

### P5 — OUTCOME
Commercial result captured.

Classify:
- WON = attributed sale/repeat/upsell;
- PROGRESSED = meaningful response/checkout/commitment without completed sale;
- NO SALE = action executed but no commercial result;
- REJECTED = creator rejected recommendation;
- INVALID = experiment compromised or attribution impossible.

### P6 — DECISION
Every pilot must end with one decision:
- EXPAND;
- ITERATE;
- NURTURE;
- STOP.

No pilot may remain indefinitely in a vague "interesting" state.

## 2. Commercial evidence standard

Evidence hierarchy, strongest first:

1. payment/purchase record linked to Mara-originated action;
2. checkout or purchase confirmation plus creator confirmation;
3. creator-provided sales evidence plus timestamp/action match;
4. customer commitment or measurable progression;
5. creator-reported workflow value;
6. qualitative preference only.

Mara must never describe level 4–6 evidence as proven incremental revenue.

## 3. Incremental revenue attribution

For each WON outcome record:

- gross revenue generated;
- offer sold;
- whether customer was already in an active sales conversation;
- whether creator had independently planned the same action;
- creator assessment: would this sale likely have happened without Mara? YES / NO / UNCERTAIN;
- Mara attribution confidence: HIGH / MEDIUM / LOW;
- evidence reference.

### Attribution rule

Count `Mara-attributed GMV` only when:

- Mara produced or materially prioritized the opportunity before the action;
- the creator acted because of the recommendation;
- a commercial result followed within a defined attribution window;
- there is no stronger conflicting explanation.

Use conservative attribution. Under-claiming is preferable to fake proof.

## 4. Pilot-level KPIs

Required:

- time to first opportunity;
- opportunities generated;
- opportunities approved;
- approval rate;
- actions executed;
- action rate;
- positive responses;
- checkouts created;
- purchases generated;
- gross GMV influenced;
- Mara-attributed GMV;
- second purchases generated;
- creator minutes spent;
- usefulness score 1–10;
- continue score 1–10;
- willingness to pay;
- referral produced.

## 5. Cohort KPIs

Track across the first 10 creators:

### Acquisition
- qualified prospects / researched prospects;
- replies / outreach attempts;
- discovery calls / replies;
- pilots started / discovery calls.

### Activation
- pilots reaching first opportunity;
- median time to first opportunity;
- creators acting on at least one recommendation.

### Commercial value
- creators with at least one WON outcome;
- total Mara-attributed GMV;
- median attributed GMV per activated creator;
- second-purchase rate among eligible customers;
- opportunity-to-sale conversion.

### Retention proxy
- creators wanting to continue after day 7;
- creators asking for another recommendation;
- creators voluntarily sharing more customer data;
- referrals.

### Pricing signal
- willing to pay monthly;
- willing to pay performance fee;
- willing to pay hybrid;
- price objections.

## 6. Stop-loss rules

Stop or redesign a pilot when:

- creator cannot provide a measurable baseline;
- creator does not execute any recommendation after agreeing to pilot;
- recommendations are consistently obvious/no-value;
- customer data needed exceeds reasonable trust/privacy boundary;
- attribution cannot be meaningfully separated from normal creator activity;
- founder effort per creator is disproportionate to plausible value;
- the creator has no repeat/upsell economics.

Do not keep weak pilots alive to protect vanity metrics.

## 7. Product feedback classification

Every friction point must be tagged as one of:

- DATA GAP;
- UX FRICTION;
- TRUST GAP;
- OPPORTUNITY QUALITY;
- MESSAGE QUALITY;
- OFFER DESIGN;
- ATTRIBUTION GAP;
- PAYMENT GAP;
- FULFILLMENT GAP;
- NON-PRODUCT / PROCESS.

Only repeated or high-severity friction should become product work.

## 8. Founder operating cadence

### Daily
- update every active pilot stage;
- close stale opportunity records;
- capture outcome evidence immediately;
- record objections verbatim;
- identify one highest-leverage next action per creator.

### Twice weekly
Review:
- strongest opportunity pattern;
- weakest recommendation pattern;
- creator segment with fastest activation;
- revenue evidence quality;
- founder time cost.

### After creators 1–5
Mandatory wedge review:
- which segment activates fastest?
- which segment has clearest repeat economics?
- where is attributed revenue easiest to prove?
- where does creator trust form fastest?

Creators 6–10 should concentrate around the strongest emerging wedge rather than preserve artificial diversity.

## 9. Pilot exit memo

Every completed pilot gets a short memo:

- creator archetype;
- problem tested;
- baseline;
- opportunities produced;
- actions executed;
- commercial outcomes;
- attributed GMV;
- strongest evidence;
- strongest objection;
- product friction;
- willingness to continue/pay;
- recommendation: EXPAND / ITERATE / NURTURE / STOP.

## 10. First proof threshold

Mara should not claim product-market fit from the first sale.

A meaningful early proof pattern begins when multiple similar creators independently show:

1. fast activation;
2. action on Mara recommendations;
3. measurable repeat/upsell revenue;
4. desire to continue;
5. economically plausible willingness to pay.

The target is not applause.

The target is a repeatable commercial pattern.
