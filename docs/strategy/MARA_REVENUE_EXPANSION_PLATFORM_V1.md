# `ECONOMIC INFRASTRUCTURE FOR DIGITAL PERSONAS`

The core opportunity is not to maximize the commission on one fan-to-creator transaction.

The stronger company coordinates and monetizes economic activity among:

- creators;
- fans;
- characters;
- products;
- agencies;
- brands;
- developers;
- commerce partners.

The same creator/character/fan graph should be capable of producing several revenue streams without proportional founder labor.

Mara should increasingly monetize **measurable value created**:

- when Mara creates demand, Mara can earn more;
- when Mara increases creator revenue, Mara can share in verified incremental value;
- when Mara saves creator/agency operating time, software revenue is justified;
- when brands access qualified character inventory, Mara can earn marketplace fees;
- when third parties build useful creator tools, Mara can earn ecosystem revenue.

This creates a more defensible revenue stack than a single marketplace take rate.

## S — CORE ECONOMIC ENGINES

### S1. Source-aware marketplace take

Why:

- directly tied to GMV;
- easy to explain;
- aligns with marketplace demand creation;
- scales with creator success;
- foundational to attribution economics.

Rule:

**Creator-sourced demand should not automatically pay the same economics as Mara-sourced demand.**

Pricing remains experimental until actual provider costs are known.

### S2. Creator Pro

Why:

- platform MRR independent of one fan purchase;
- supports BYOA economics;
- creator tooling can materially raise earnings/hour;
- lowers dependence on marketplace take.

Pro must sell outcomes and operating leverage, not charts.

### S3. Mara Growth / Revenue Copilot

Why:

- strongest incentive alignment;
- converts dormant creator inventory into revenue;
- can justify performance pricing;
- produces measurable creator value;
- deepens the data/merchandising moat.

Must not charge performance fees without auditable attribution.

### S4. Personalized products + bounded creator-time inventory

Why:

- high willingness to pay;
- natural scarcity;
- does not require increasing explicitness;
- often higher creator earnings/hour than live open-ended interaction;
- improves marketplace differentiation.

Examples:

- personalized audio;
- personalized message;
- limited custom-product slots;
- bounded 15/30/60 minute interaction products where supported.

### S5. Creator Privacy tooling

Privacy is not only a trust feature; it is a reason the target creator may choose Mara at all.

Possible monetization:

- included in Pro;
- premium checks/transformations;
- advanced Privacy Pro tier later.

The product must never promise perfect anonymity.

# D. MARA GROWTH — THE MOST IMPORTANT NEW ECONOMIC LAYER

## Definition

Mara Growth should answer:

> **What incremental revenue can Mara help this creator create using inventory, demand and relationships that already exist?**

Creator uploads/owns inventory.

Mara may identify:

- unused assets;
- incomplete collections;
- audience demand signals;
- dormant buyers;
- repeat-purchase patterns;
- unfilled personalization demand;
- available creator-time inventory;
- collaboration opportunities.

Then Mara proposes an executable commercial action.

Example:

`27 UNUSED ASSETS`

→ `3 POSSIBLE BUNDLES`

→ `TARGET SEGMENT`

→ `PRICE HYPOTHESIS`

→ `PREVIEW`

→ `CAMPAIGN`

→ `CREATOR APPROVAL`

→ `ATTRIBUTED SALES`

Mara can charge only when the economic benefit is transparent.

## Possible pricing tests

### Growth included in Creator Pro

Simple, low friction.

### Growth Plus

Monthly fee for more campaign volume/automation.

### Performance fee

Example hypothesis only:

`5–10% of verified incremental creator revenue attributable to a Mara Growth campaign.`

Do not use the performance model until attribution integrity is good enough to prevent creator distrust.

# E. MARA GROWTH ATTRIBUTION CONTRACT

A sale occurring after a Mara recommendation is not automatically Mara-created revenue.

Every Growth campaign should eventually have:

- `campaign_id`;
- `creator_account_id`;
- `character_id`;
- `campaign_type`;
- `target_segment_definition`;
- `baseline_period`;
- `attribution_start_at`;
- `attribution_end_at`;
- `source_kind`;
- `offer_ids`;
- `holdout/control_definition` where practical;
- `gross_attributed_gmv`;
- `refund_adjusted_gmv`;
- `creator_net_attributed_earnings`;
- `estimated_incremental_gmv`;
- `Mara_growth_fee`;
- `attribution_rule_version`;
- `explanation`.

## Attribution levels

### DIRECT

Unique campaign link / campaign-specific offer / explicit campaign purchase path.

Confidence: high.

### ASSISTED

User was exposed to Mara Growth merchandising and later purchased within a defined window.

Confidence: medium.

### INCREMENTAL ESTIMATE

Statistical comparison against baseline/holdout.

Confidence must be disclosed.

Never silently bill an incremental-performance fee on ambiguous assisted attribution.

# W. UNIT ECONOMICS

Never use headline take rate as margin.

Core formula:

`MARA REVENUE - PROCESSING/PAYOUT - FRAUD/CHARGEBACKS - REFUNDS - AI/MEDIA - SUPPORT - MODERATION - COMPLIANCE - ATTRIBUTED CAC = CONTRIBUTION MARGIN`

## Scenario 1 — creator-sourced digital product

Illustrative only:

GMV: $100.

Platform take: 12–15%.

If total variable platform costs reach 10%, contribution is only $2–5 before fixed cost.

Implication:

**BYOA needs either efficient processing/support economics or Creator Pro SaaS.**

## Scenario 2 — Mara-sourced digital product

GMV: $100.

Platform take hypothesis: 22–28%.

If variable platform costs are 10%, gross contribution before acquisition amortization is $12–18.

Then subtract actual attributable CAC.

## Scenario 3 — Creator Pro

$49/month subscription.

Variable cost depends on included:

- AI inference;
- storage;
- voice/localization;
- support.

Protect margin with usage limits rather than unlimited expensive AI/media generation.

## Scenario 4 — Growth performance fee

$300 estimated incremental GMV.

7% Growth fee = $21.

This is attractive only when:

- attribution is trusted;
- incremental campaign execution cost is low;
- creator perceives clear net value.

## Scenario 5 — Agency OS

$249/month with 15 managed creators = ~$16.60 SaaS revenue per managed creator before support/infra.

Additional transaction economics may materially improve value, but double-charging must be transparent.

## Scenario 6 — Brand marketplace

$10,000 campaign.

20% platform/managed fee = $2,000 gross Mara revenue.

This can be high-value, but sales and campaign-management labor can destroy margin if not standardized.

Translate top-selling storefront/products before generating new inventory.

Sell/install standardized workflows for launches, fulfillment, drops and campaigns.

A standardized paid onboarding package can monetize creator setup without a perpetual managed-service burden.

## 10. Brand-safe character certification

A future brand marketplace needs clear inventory categorization and campaign suitability.

## 11. Rights-expiry management

Brand/voice/content licenses require automatic expiry windows; Rights OS could become valuable Agency/Studio tooling.

## 12. Digital merchandise

Wallpapers, sticker packs, emoji/reaction packs, character assets and collectible non-random digital products.

## 13. Physical merchandise via partners

Strong characters can monetize without Mara holding inventory using print-on-demand partners.

## 14. Character franchise templates

Mara Originals may eventually license specific fictional worlds/templates to verified operators under explicit rights contracts.

## 15. Campaign holdouts as trust infrastructure

Growth performance fees become more credible when Mara can show an actual comparison group rather than a marketing attribution claim.

## 16. Revenue-share caps

Performance fees may need monthly caps to keep creator economics predictable and reduce resentment.

## 17. Creator profitability alerts

"This product sells well but consumes too much of your time" is more valuable than revenue-only analytics.

## 18. Fulfillment SLAs

Creators can sell standard vs priority delivery with clearly priced service levels.

## 19. Waitlist conversion

A waitlist is not just UX; it measures unsatisfied demand and supports capacity/pricing decisions.

## 20. Collaboration matching

Mara can recommend economically complementary creators based on audience/product overlap without exposing sensitive fan data.

## 21. Agency talent scouting

Once creator metrics mature, agencies may use Mara to identify reliable creators — but creators need explicit visibility/control over discoverability.

## 22. Brand demand forecasting

Aggregated, privacy-safe demand can show brands which character niches/categories are growing.

## 23. Creator benchmark subscription for agencies

Aggregated market benchmarks can be Agency Pro value once samples are large enough.

## 24. Marketplace liquidity score

Measure whether a creator actually has enough buyer demand, rather than optimizing creator count.

## 25. Product-level reputation

Fulfillment quality may belong to the SKU/product rather than a simplistic public five-star rating of a creator.

## 26. Exit-safe entitlements

Fan purchases need continuity rules if a creator leaves; this increases trust and reduces dispute risk.

## 27. Rights-safe resale is mostly a trap

Do not assume digital character assets should become tradable. Resale creates rights, securities/speculation and consumer complexity without proving core value.

## 28. Creator insurance-like products are risky

"Chargeback protection" can be positioned as tooling/support, but underwriting financial loss may become an insurance/regulatory business. Do not promise coverage casually.

## 29. Financing creators is seductive but premature

Advances against future earnings could generate fees but introduce credit/regulatory risk and conflict with a low-maintenance founder model.

## 30. Category expansion requires brand architecture

If Mara later serves mainstream lifestyle creators, the company may need strong content-category walls/sub-brands so the initial adult wedge does not block payment, advertiser or creator expansion.
