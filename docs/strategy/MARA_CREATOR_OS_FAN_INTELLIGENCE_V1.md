# A. FOUNDER-LEVEL STRATEGIC CONCLUSION

Mara should not become merely a marketplace, a creator storefront or a CRM.

The stronger company is:

> **A creator operating system that helps verified adult creators earn more per hour from digital personas while Mara provides privacy, commerce, demand, relationship intelligence and trust infrastructure.**

The core economic problem is not simply `how do we stop creators exchanging Instagram handles?`.

The correct problem is:

`VALUE OF STAYING ON MARA > SAVINGS FROM BYPASSING MARA`

for both creator and fan.

The defensible layer is therefore the combination of:

- creator-controlled public pseudonymity;
- fan-side privacy;
- transaction infrastructure;
- source attribution;
- repeat-purchase history;
- creator-side relationship intelligence;
- merchandising recommendations;
- bounded creator-time inventory;
- character management;
- cross-creator discovery;
- payment/trust protections;
- measurable marketplace demand.

Mara must not claim ownership of creators or fans. It must earn retention by continuing to perform economically valuable work.

## P0 decision

The next product proof should be creator-side:

`CREATOR DASHBOARD → FAN 360 → SOURCE ATTRIBUTION → NEXT BEST ACTION`

But the first implementation remains an internal DEV-only lab using privacy-safe synthetic data.

Why this is the correct P0:

1. the repository already has Creator Zero commerce primitives, purchases, entitlements and user preference events;
2. a second consumer feature does not test the new marketplace thesis;
3. full creator accounts/payouts are blocked by verification/payment-provider diligence;
4. a Creator OS lab can validate the information architecture and economics without pretending real creator infrastructure exists;
5. deterministic recommendations can be tested before spending on LLM inference or autonomous messaging.

Do **not** implement real creator-facing Fan 360 until creator authorization, data-scoping and real marketplace commerce exist.

# C. FAN INTELLIGENCE ARCHITECTURE

## Fan 360 principle

Fan 360 is a **creator-scoped commercial relationship view**, not a dossier.

Each field must be classifiable as:

### DECLARED

The fan explicitly provided it.

Example:

`Preferred format: audio`

Source: user preference control.

### OBSERVED

A literal transaction/event occurred.

Example:

`3 of last 4 purchases were audio products.`

### DERIVED

A transparent rule transformed declared/observed data.

Example:

`Likely audio buyer — confidence high because 75% of recent purchases were audio.`

Derived signals must include an explanation and confidence. They are suggestions, not facts about the person's psychology.

## Fan 360 commercial fields

Candidate fields:

- Fan Alias;
- Relationship age;
- First purchase;
- Last purchase;
- Total spend;
- Creator net earnings;
- Order count;
- AOV;
- Purchase frequency;
- Products purchased;
- Formats purchased;
- Membership status;
- Session history;
- Personalized-product status;
- Refund history;
- Creator time consumed;
- Earnings per creator hour;
- Attribution source;
- Campaign/referral source;
- Last meaningful interaction;
- Declared format preference;
- Declared language;
- Declared interaction preference;
- Declared boundaries where needed for fulfillment;
- Offer acceptance/rejection history;
- Dormancy;
- Next-best product;
- Next-best action.

## Relationship Value

Do not rank customers only by total spend.

Track creator-time economics.

Example:

- Fan A: $150 creator revenue / 180 creator minutes.
- Fan B: $110 creator revenue / 20 creator minutes.

Fan B may deliver materially better earnings per creator hour.

Recommended creator-facing metrics:

- creator net earnings;
- creator minutes consumed;
- creator earnings / hour;
- repeat-purchase count;
- relationship recency;
- refund/support cost where known.

Do not present pseudo-scientific lifetime-value certainty.

A Mara-sourced relationship may justify front-loaded economics because Mara paid/accrued cost to create the match.

Alternative: a fixed acquisition premium rather than changing percentages.

Do not ship commission decay until creators understand it instantly and unit economics prove it reduces leakage rather than creating pricing confusion.

`GMV - CREATOR PAYOUT - PAYMENT/PAYOUT COST - FRAUD/CHARGEBACK RESERVE - REFUNDS - SUPPORT - MODERATION - STORAGE/CDN - AI/VOICE/MEDIA COST - COMPLIANCE = MARA CONTRIBUTION MARGIN`

## Illustrative $100 GMV scenarios — hypotheses only

These are not provider quotes.

Assume combined variable non-creator costs of 8–12% in an adult/high-risk creator context until actual provider contracts prove otherwise.

### Creator-sourced Starter at 12% take

Platform gross revenue: $12.

At 8% variable platform cost: $4 contribution before fixed overhead.

At 12% variable platform cost: approximately break-even.

Conclusion:

> A very low creator-sourced take is dangerous unless Creator Pro subscription revenue or lower verified processing/support costs compensate for it.

This only works if the monthly SaaS fee covers enough infrastructure/fixed contribution and creator GMV is sufficiently high.

### Mara-sourced at 25%

Platform gross revenue: $25.

At 10% variable platform cost: $15 contribution before acquisition amortization and fixed overhead.

Then subtract CAC/acquisition attribution to determine whether Mara-sourced demand is actually profitable.

- sexual orientation;

# H. VOICE + CHARACTER IDENTITY ARCHITECTURE

Voice can become a privacy tool and character layer, but it is not P0.

Potential modes:

1. PERSONAL VOICE — creator publishes original voice.
2. PROCESSED VOICE — cleanup/pitch/timbre transformation within creator-authorized preset.
3. CHARACTER VOICE — creator-approved synthetic/transformed identity.
4. MULTILINGUAL VOICE — creator-authorized translation/dubbing.

Rules:

- explicit creator authorization;
- disclosure appropriate to the surface;
- no cloning unrelated third-party identifiable voices;
- voice presets attached to creator-owned/authorized character identity;
- revoke/disable voice assets when rights are withdrawn;
- preserve source/consent evidence privately.

## Multi-character economics

One verified creator may operate a portfolio.

Benefits:

- multiple market positions from one operational account;
- segmentation without exposing civil identity;
- reuse of creator time/inventory;
- higher potential revenue per creator.

Risks:

- misleading fans;
- operational overload;
- content-rights ambiguity;
- cross-character identity leakage;
- reputation concentration;
- fraud/ban evasion.

Therefore multi-character is P2/P3, not pilot P0.

Not public five-star ratings necessarily; internal fulfillment reliability and abuse risk can protect quality.
