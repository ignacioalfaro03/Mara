# 1. FOUNDER DECISION

Mara is a **creator commerce platform for people whose audience wants to get closer to them**.

The primary market is not “everyone who has an identity online”. The initial commercial ICP is people who already generate, or can plausibly generate:

- desire;
- curiosity;
- admiration;
- fandom;
- parasocial proximity;
- willingness to pay for access, recognition, personalization or exclusivity.

The primary supply will therefore be creator-led:

- content creators;
- influencers and microinfluencers;
- models;
- streamers;
- adult creators where payment/content policy permits;
- personality-led social accounts;
- creators with an existing audience that monetizes poorly;
- people who prefer to operate through a character, alias or virtual identity.

Other professionals may use the infrastructure later, but they must not distort the initial product.

Core economic formula:

`CREATOR × FAN × PERMITTED INTERACTION → TRANSACTION → REPEAT PURCHASE`

The core question is:

> **Is there an audience willing to pay to feel closer to this creator?**

If yes, Mara should make that willingness easy to package, price, purchase, fulfill and repeat.

---

# 2. EXPOSURE IS A SETTING, NOT THE PRODUCT

Mara must support creators who want to appear publicly and creators who do not.

Three primary public identity modes:

## REAL ME

The creator uses her real public creator identity and image.

Best fit:
- influencer;
- public content creator;
- model;
- streamer;
- creator already comfortable showing herself.

Mara adds monetization infrastructure, not concealment.

## CREATOR PERSONA

The creator may use her real image/content but under a distinct creator brand, stage name or commercial persona.

## VIRTUAL IDENTITY

The creator operates primarily through a character/avatar/alias and keeps more of her real-world identity private from fans.

Mara Vera is Creator Zero for this mode.

These are not separate products. They are exposure configurations inside the same commerce system.

---

# 3. THE CORE PRODUCT IS A CREATOR STORE

Every creator receives a public commerce surface such as:

`mara.com/@creator`

This is not merely a profile or feed. It is a **storefront for personal access and creator-defined offers**.

The creator can create any permitted Offer with:

- title;
- description;
- price;
- currency;
- type;
- delivery method;
- delivery deadline;
- quantity/capacity;
- availability;
- visibility;
- buyer requirements;
- cancellation/refund policy;
- creator-time requirement;
- fulfillment state;
- attribution source;
- entitlement where applicable.

Canonical creation flow:

`CREATE → CHOOSE WHAT I SELL → SET PRICE → SET LIMITS → PUBLISH`

The default UX must make charging easy.

A creator may also set price = 0 when a free offer is useful for acquisition or retention.

A creator-defined desire or goal that fans may fund.

# 5. CAPRICHOS

Caprichos monetizes participation in the creator’s world.

Examples:

- coffee today;
- flowers;
- dinner;
- a dress;
- a pair of shoes;
- a new phone;
- equipment for content;
- a creator-defined goal.

A Capricho may be funded by one fan or collectively.

Suggested object model:

- creator_id;
- character_id;
- title;
- description;
- target_amount;
- funded_amount;
- contribution_minimum;
- contributor_visibility;
- deadline optional;
- fulfillment/evidence state;
- status;
- platform fee rule;
- linked content/update optional.

Canonical loop:

`DESIRE → CONTRIBUTION → FUNDING → OUTCOME/UPDATE → NEW DESIRE`

Important distinction:

A Capricho does **not** automatically promise a product or service in exchange for the contribution.

If a concrete deliverable is promised, it must be modeled as an Offer with explicit fulfillment terms.

This distinction matters for user expectation, disputes, accounting and policy.

# 6. BRING YOUR AUDIENCE

Mara should not ask creators to rebuild their social graph from zero.

The commercial promise is:

> **Keep growing where your audience already lives. Bring the traffic to Mara and monetize it here.**

External social platforms remain creator acquisition channels.

Mara becomes the conversion, commerce and retention layer.

Every creator should receive source-aware links:

- Instagram;
- TikTok;
- X;
- YouTube;
- Twitch;
- Reddit;
- Telegram;
- custom campaign;
- direct;
- Mara Discovery.

Required attribution chain:

`SOURCE → VISIT → SIGNUP/IDENTITY STATE → FIRST PURCHASE → GMV → REPEAT GMV`

Creator-facing analytics should answer:

- which channel sends the most visitors;
- which channel converts best;
- which channel produces the highest GMV/visitor;
- which channel produces the most repeat buyers;
- how much GMV was creator-sourced;
- how much GMV was Mara-sourced.

This is not vanity analytics. It is creator business intelligence.

# 7. PRICING BY DEMAND SOURCE

A creator who brings her own buyer should not be priced identically to a creator whose buyer Mara acquired.

Initial hypothesis only:

## CREATOR-SOURCED / BYOA

Lower effective platform take.

Reason: Mara supplied infrastructure, conversion, trust, delivery, CRM and analytics, but not the initial audience acquisition.

## MARA-SOURCED

Higher effective platform take.

Reason: Mara supplied discovery/demand in addition to infrastructure.

Simple creator-facing principle:

> **Bring your audience, pay less. Mara brings the buyer, Mara earns more.**

Exact pricing remains subject to actual payment, payout, fraud, support, moderation and compliance costs.

Do not promise a low take rate before contribution margin is proven.

# 8. FAN CRM + REPEAT PURCHASE

The business does not end at checkout.

Mara should help creators understand their creator-scoped commercial relationships without exposing unnecessary private information.

Useful signals include:

- public fan alias;
- order history;
- creator net revenue;
- favorite purchased format based on literal behavior;
- membership state;
- pending fulfillment;
- source attribution;
- recency;
- repeat-purchase count;
- creator time consumed;
- earnings per creator hour;
- transparent next-best action.

Mara must not build psychological dossiers or infer sensitive vulnerabilities to increase spend.

The long-term advantage is:

`FIRST PURCHASE → GOOD FULFILLMENT → RELEVANT NEXT OFFER → SECOND PURCHASE → RETAINED FAN`

---

# 9. MARA AUTOSELL

The creator should not need to be online constantly to monetize.

Mara may eventually act as a deterministic/AI-assisted commerce copilot that helps:

- turn existing content into sellable SKUs;
- recommend prices to test;
- recommend bundles;
- identify unfinished collections;
- surface pending fulfillment;
- identify channel conversion differences;
- draft creator-approved offers;
- draft reactivation messages;
- recommend membership only after repeat behavior exists;
- recommend a Capricho when the creator explicitly wants to publish one.

Mara should never silently send manipulative sales messages or fabricate creator actions.

Creator control remains explicit.

# 13. PAYMENT + TRUST GATE

Do not activate real payments merely because the UI or API works.

The actual Mara model may include:

- third-party creators;
- creator payouts;
- adult-oriented digital content/services;
- tips/contributions;
- personalized content;
- recurring memberships;
- marketplace discovery.

These materially affect processor eligibility, KYC, payout, tax, moderation and card-network obligations.

Permanent gate:

> **NO REAL-MONEY ACTIVATION UNTIL THE PROVIDER EXPLICITLY SUPPORTS THE ACTUAL BUSINESS MODEL, CONTENT CATEGORIES, CREATOR PAYOUT STRUCTURE AND TARGET GEOGRAPHIES.**

MVP architecture should therefore use provider abstraction and avoid embedding a single unsupported processor assumption.

# 14. PRODUCT FILTER

Every major feature should answer at least one of these:

1. Does it help a creator acquire a buyer?
2. Does it increase conversion?
3. Does it increase creator GMV?
4. Does it improve fulfillment?
5. Does it increase repeat purchase?
6. Does it increase earnings per creator hour?
7. Does it increase trust/privacy enough to retain creator or fan?

If the answer is no, it is probably not core.

# 16. FOUNDER SUMMARY

Mara is not a generic identity platform.

Mara is not only for anonymous creators.

Mara is not only for creators who show themselves.

Mara is not primarily a feed.

Mara is the **commerce layer for creator-fan proximity**.

A creator chooses how much of herself is public, chooses what she is willing to sell, chooses the price and limits, brings her existing audience if she has one, and uses Mara to convert attention into revenue.

Mara earns by making that commerce easier, safer, more measurable and more repeatable — and earns more when Mara itself creates the demand.
