# MARA — INSTAGRAM FEED SYSTEM V1

Status: strategy draft for founder-led validation.  
Date: 2026-09-12.  
Authority: subordinate to `MARA_FOUNDATIONAL_THESIS.md`.

## Strategic role of Instagram

Instagram is not a vanity channel for Mara. It is a demand-generation and category-memory system for creators who already use social platforms as their operating environment.

The account should make a creator think one of four things before they ever click the bio:

1. **That happens to me.**
2. **I am probably leaving money on the table.**
3. **These people understand creator commerce better than generic SaaS companies do.**
4. **I want Mara to look at my business.**

The feed does not exist to explain every feature. It exists to create and reinforce memory structures around buying situations where Mara should be recalled.

Core buying situations / Category Entry Points to repeatedly own:

- "I have followers but do not know who will buy."
- "I made one sale; now what?"
- "My DMs contain people asking about prices / availability / offers."
- "People buy once and disappear."
- "I am launching something and do not know whom to contact first."
- "I hate spamming everyone."
- "I have several products but do not know what to offer each customer."
- "I have no idea which customers are actually valuable."
- "My business lives in Instagram, WhatsApp, spreadsheets and memory."
- "I know I am probably losing sales, but cannot see where."

The repeated association should become:

`REVENUE LEAK / COMMERCIAL GUESSWORK → MARA`

and later:

`SECOND SALE / NEXT BEST ACTION → MARA`

## Positioning implication

Do not make the Instagram account look like a generic AI startup.

No visual language built around:

- purple gradients;
- glowing AI brains;
- fake 3D robots;
- random futuristic renders;
- generic laptop mockups floating in space;
- synthetic smiling creators;
- "10 AI hacks" carousels;
- ChatGPT screenshots as content;
- meaningless productivity aphorisms;
- constant feature announcements;
- template-heavy Canva sameness.

The market category should orient the buyer, but differentiated value must be explicit. Mara can call itself a Creator Revenue OS, but the feed must demonstrate *why Mara exists*: creator businesses lose revenue because customer memory, commercial context and next actions are fragmented.

## Editorial identity

Mara should feel like the intersection of:

- a serious business publication;
- a modern creator-culture magazine;
- a revenue operator who has actually looked inside creator businesses;
- a product company with taste.

Not "startup Instagram".

Working editorial characteristics:

- sharp;
- commercially literate;
- observant;
- restrained;
- slightly provocative;
- evidence-led;
- human;
- culturally current;
- never motivational-speaker energy.

Voice:

- short declarative headlines;
- real creator language;
- concrete commercial situations;
- no jargon unless immediately translated;
- no inflated claims;
- no "revolutionize", "unlock your potential", "supercharge", "game-changing", "AI-powered growth";
- no fake certainty when evidence is weak.

## Visual doctrine

### Principle 1 — Distinctive before decorative

The feed must be recognizable without the logo.

Distinctive assets should compound instead of being redesigned every week. The system should select and hold:

- one primary typeface family;
- one secondary editorial typeface if needed;
- one narrow set of recurring layout geometries;
- one photographic treatment;
- one iconographic language;
- one small set of recurring brand marks / devices;
- one consistent information hierarchy.

Avoid building recognition only from a logo or one color.

### Principle 2 — Real imagery beats synthetic imagery

Use, in order of preference:

1. original photography of real creators / operators / workspaces;
2. documentary screenshots of real commercial workflows, anonymized where needed;
3. real customer artifacts: checkout, DM, spreadsheet, notes, receipts, storefronts, analytics;
4. commissioned editorial illustration with a distinctive human style;
5. product UI shown only when it demonstrates a real decision or proof.

Generative imagery should be exceptional rather than default. If an image could be identified immediately as generic AI art, do not publish it.

### Principle 3 — The feed is not a checkerboard template

Do not force a rigid "quote / photo / carousel / quote" grid merely for aesthetic symmetry.

Each individual post must work in-feed and in isolation when discovered through Explore, shares or Reels.

The grid should still feel coherent through typography, cropping, margins, recurring marks and editorial tone.

### Principle 4 — Editorial tension

Use contrast between:

- clean, quiet information cards;
- raw documentary creator-business evidence;
- strong portraiture;
- close-up objects from creator commerce;
- highly specific data / annotated breakdowns.

This prevents the account from looking like 100 consecutive SaaS carousels.

## Feed architecture

Target mix over a rolling 20-feed-post window:

- **30% Revenue Leaks / Recognition** — situations that make the creator self-identify.
- **20% Commercial Education** — teach a mental model creators can use immediately.
- **20% Proof / Teardowns / Cases** — real audits, before-after decisions, measurable outcomes.
- **15% Creator Business Culture** — portraits, field notes, interviews, operating realities.
- **10% Mara Product in Context** — show Mara solving a specific decision, never feature dumps.
- **5% Company / Founder / Build-in-public evidence** — only when it increases trust or explains a decision.

Do not maintain these percentages mechanically. They are a portfolio guardrail.

## The six recurring editorial franchises

### 1. YOU ARE LOSING THIS SALE

Purpose: category-entry-point memory + problem recognition.

Format: single strong image or 3–5-slide carousel.

Examples:

- "She bought from you 41 days ago. You never spoke to her again."
- "Three people asked the price. You answered. Then nothing."
- "Your best customer may look identical to a follower in Instagram."
- "You launched to 18,000 followers. But who actually needed the offer?"

Visual treatment:

- documentary crop of DMs / transaction timeline / simple annotated timeline;
- one sentence as headline;
- small Mara mark;
- no decorative filler.

CTA: usually no hard sell. Caption ends with a diagnostic question.

### 2. THE SECOND SALE

Purpose: make Mara mentally own repeat purchase.

This should become a flagship franchise.

Topics:

- why first purchase is not the end of a funnel;
- timing between purchases;
- what the first purchase teaches you;
- why a buyer should not return to the anonymous follower pool;
- next best offer;
- reactivation;
- customer memory.

Recurring conceptual line:

> The first sale proves demand. The second sale starts a business relationship.

Do not overuse the exact sentence. Own the idea, not one slogan.

### 3. REVENUE AUTOPSY

Purpose: show operator competence.

Take a real or permissioned creator commerce setup and break down where money may be leaking.

Structure:

1. What the creator sells.
2. Where buyers enter.
3. What happens after intent.
4. What happens after purchase.
5. Leakage points.
6. Three actions we would test.

Never fabricate revenue numbers.

If data is incomplete, label hypotheses clearly.

This franchise can become a lead magnet: "Send us your setup; we may audit it."

### 4. CREATOR BUSINESS NOTES

Purpose: sound like the closest observer of creator businesses, not a vendor shouting at them.

Examples:

- annotated notebook page from a creator launch;
- how one creator actually tracks customers today;
- 5 screenshots that describe the real operating stack;
- "the spreadsheet behind a 70K-follower account";
- short interview: "What happens after someone buys from you?"

Photography should be natural, editorial and specific to the subject.

### 5. MARA FOUND IT

Purpose: product proof.

Each post begins with one detected commercial situation, not a feature name.

Bad:

> Introducing Opportunity Engine.

Good:

> 17 customers bought this offer. 6 are entering the same repurchase window this week.

Then show Mara surfacing the action.

The product is the reveal, not the headline.

### 6. NO, YOU DO NOT NEED TO...

Purpose: counter-positioning and point of view.

Examples:

- "No, you do not need to DM every follower."
- "No, another link-in-bio will not remember your customers."
- "No, more views do not tell you who is ready to buy."
- "No, every buyer should not receive the same offer."

This creates a recognizable strategic point of view without cheap rage bait.

## Format system

### Static single-image posts

Use when one sentence is the idea.

Rules:

- one dominant thought;
- 5–14 words is ideal for the visual headline;
- caption carries nuance;
- avoid tiny body copy on the image;
- imagery must add meaning, not merely background texture.

### Carousels

Use for arguments, teardowns and frameworks.

Default structure:

1. Recognition / tension.
2. Concrete example.
3. Why it happens.
4. Commercial consequence.
5. Better model.
6. Action / question.

Not every carousel needs 10 slides. Stop when the argument is finished.

Do not title carousels "5 tips to..." unless enumeration genuinely improves the idea.

### Reels

Reels should create reach without lowering taste.

Preferred types:

- founder/operator speaking directly to camera with one commercial observation;
- documentary micro-story inside a creator business;
- screen + voice analysis of a real monetization flow;
- short creator interview;
- teardown on paper / whiteboard / screen recording;
- one strong claim supported by evidence.

Avoid:

- stock B-roll plus AI voice;
- kinetic captions over random city footage;
- faceless "guru" reels;
- trending audio added without conceptual relevance;
- synthetic avatars;
- empty hook formulas.

Reel opening should often be a creator situation rather than a meta hook.

Example:

> "If 200 people have already paid you, why are you still treating them like anonymous followers?"

### Stories

Stories are the laboratory and conversation layer.

Daily/near-daily uses when active:

- polls around actual commercial behavior;
- screenshots from founder research;
- mini audits;
- questions to creators;
- launch / pilot recruitment;
- behind-the-scenes product evidence;
- follow-up to feed arguments;
- reposts of creator responses / case evidence with permission.

Stories can be less polished than feed but not careless.

7. **Revenue Autopsy #001** — teardown.

11. **Portrait / interview: how do you remember who bought what?** — culture.
12. **The Second Sale #002** — repurchase timing.

## Visual direction for the first feed

Working direction: **commercial editorial / documentary modernism**.

Visual ingredients:

- warm or neutral real-world photography;
- black-and-white used selectively for documentary authority;
- close crops of phones, notebooks, shipping, creator setups, payment screens, packaging, studio details;
- large typographic headlines with generous whitespace;
- visible annotations / circles / underlines where diagnostic;
- tables, timelines and simple charts when they reveal money or behavior;
- product UI captured cleanly in context;
- occasional full-bleed portrait.

Avoid looking like a finance company, marketing agency or luxury-fashion moodboard. Editorial taste must serve creator commerce.

A useful reference axis is not to copy a specific grid, but to combine:

- the recognizability and strong typography of editorial business content;
- the humanity of creator portraiture;
- the evidence density of an analyst's notebook;
- the restraint of a serious software brand.

## Photography brief

When original shoots begin, shoot horizontal and vertical variants.

Scenes worth building a proprietary image library around:

- creator at desk answering DMs;
- hands on phone with inbox / orders;
- product packing / physical merch;
- creator recording content;
- editing timeline / camera / microphone;
- notebook with product launch plan;
- printed customer/order sheets where privacy-safe;
- small studio / bedroom / gym / salon / kitchen depending on vertical;
- checkout happening on mobile;
- creator reviewing sales with Mara;
- portrait after work, not staged "laptop smile" stock poses.

Human imperfection is welcome: cables, messy notes, real spaces, real work.

## Copy system

### Headline grammar

Prefer:

- observation;
- question;
- contradiction;
- specific commercial consequence;
- diagnostic statement.

Examples:

- "You answered the DM. You did not create a customer."
- "Someone bought. What did you learn?"
- "You have a content strategy. Where is your customer strategy?"
- "Your biggest fan and your best customer are not necessarily the same person."
- "A buyer should not disappear back into your follower count."
- "More reach cannot fix forgotten customers."
- "The sale happened. Your system learned nothing."

Avoid:

- "3 ways to maximize engagement";
- "The future of the creator economy is here";
- "AI changes everything";
- "Creators, this one's for you";
- fake urgency;
- emojis as visual crutches;
- hashtags stuffed for reach.

### Caption architecture

Default:

1. first sentence extends the tension rather than repeating the artwork;
2. 2–5 short paragraphs with real reasoning;
3. one concrete example;
4. one question / action.

Captions may be longer for teardowns and field notes. The account should reward serious reading.

## Calls to action

The CTA hierarchy should progress with trust.

Low-trust / early:

- "Does this happen in your business?"
- "How are you handling this today?"
- "Save this before your next launch."
- "Send this to the person who runs your DMs."

Medium-trust:

- "Send us your current flow and we will tell you where we would look first."
- "Comment AUDIT and we will send the framework" only if operationally real and not gimmicky.

High-trust / proof established:

- "Apply for a Mara Revenue Audit."
- "Join the 7-Day Revenue Sprint."

Do not force a product CTA under every post.

## Content quality gate

Before publishing, every feed post must pass all of these:

1. Would a creator recognize a real business situation here?
2. Is the idea useful even if the viewer never buys Mara?
3. Could a competitor post this unchanged? If yes, it is not distinctive enough.
4. Is there any claim we cannot substantiate?
5. Does the creative look human-made and intentionally art-directed?
6. Does it reinforce a Category Entry Point Mara wants to own?
7. Is Mara connected to the problem without hijacking the post?
8. Would we be comfortable seeing this post screenshotted without the caption?
9. Does it avoid cheap AI aesthetics and generic SaaS tropes?
10. Does it make the creator think, feel, learn or act?

If fewer than 8/10 are clearly yes, do not publish yet.

## Content production model

The system should prevent low-quality volume pressure.

Weekly target at launch:

- 2 strong feed posts;
- 1 Reel worth watching;
- Stories from ongoing founder research / pilots;
- optional third feed post only when there is something real to say.

Quality > frequency.

A backlog should be built from ongoing creator interviews and revenue audits, not brainstorming sessions detached from customers.

Content source hierarchy:

1. real creator research;
2. observed customer/revenue behavior;
3. Mara product evidence;
4. current creator-economy questions;
5. rigorous marketing / commerce theory translated into creator reality;
6. founder point of view.

Never reverse this into "we need a post Tuesday, ask AI for ideas."

## Measurement

Do not judge the feed primarily by follower count or raw likes.

Track by franchise and Category Entry Point:

- qualified profile visits;
- creator DMs;
- Revenue Audit applications;
- saves;
- shares;
- replies from target creators;
- outbound prospects mentioning a post;
- assisted pilot starts;
- attributed creator acquisition;
- which problem statements recur in discovery calls;
- unaided language: whether creators begin repeating Mara concepts such as "second sale", "revenue leak" or "I don't know who to contact".

Reach matters because mental availability requires reach, but attention without brand/problem linkage is weak.

## Brand-memory discipline

Do not redesign Mara because a new Instagram aesthetic becomes fashionable.

The feed should deliberately repeat:

- the same commercial territories;
- the same language families;
- the same visual assets;
- the same editorial franchises;
- the same basic product promise.

Consistency is not creative stagnation. The subject, photography, stories and creator businesses provide variation; the brand system provides memory.

## Current strategic hypotheses to test

H1 — `SECOND SALE` is more ownable and memorable than generic `MONETIZE YOUR AUDIENCE`.

H2 — `REVENUE LEAKAGE` produces stronger self-recognition than `CREATOR CRM`.

H3 — documentary creator-business content generates more qualified creator responses than polished SaaS product content.

H4 — real teardowns create more sales conversations than educational tip carousels.

H5 — anti-spam / selective selling messaging reduces the creator's emotional resistance to commercial software.

H6 — original creator photography + strong typography creates higher brand attribution than generic startup graphics.

These are hypotheses, not truths. Validate through creator interviews, controlled content series and downstream qualified conversion.

## Non-negotiable rule

# NO CONTENT FILLER.

If Mara has nothing true, useful, distinctive or beautifully observed to publish, Mara should not publish that day.

The feed must create the impression that the company studies creator revenue more seriously than anyone else — because the company actually does.
