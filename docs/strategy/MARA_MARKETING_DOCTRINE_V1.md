# MARA — MARKETING DOCTRINE V1

Status: working strategic doctrine
Date: 2026-09-12
Authority: subordinate to `docs/foundation/MARA_FOUNDATIONAL_THESIS.md`

## 1. Marketing objective

Mara does not need to become famous before it becomes useful. The first marketing objective is to create repeatable evidence that a specific creator profile can use Mara to generate more revenue from an audience they already own.

The sequence is:

`SPECIFIC CREATOR → SPECIFIC REVENUE PROBLEM → SPECIFIC ACTION → MEASURABLE RESULT → PROOF → REFERRAL → REPEATABLE ACQUISITION`

Marketing must sell an outcome, not a feature inventory.

## 2. Category and positioning

Working category: **Creator Revenue OS**.

Primary promise:

> Turn followers into customers. Know who to sell to next. Grow repeat revenue.

Internal positioning test:

> Mara tells a creator where the next revenue opportunity is, helps execute it, and measures whether it worked.

Avoid leading with:
- AI;
- CRM;
- link in bio;
- storefront;
- automations;
- analytics;
- all-in-one creator platform.

Those are mechanisms. Revenue improvement is the outcome.

## 3. Competitive interpretation

The creator stack is crowded with storefronts, memberships, email, funnels, auto-DM, analytics and AI assistance. Feature parity is therefore not a defensible acquisition message.

Mara should not say "we also have X" unless X removes a switching objection.

The wedge is the **commercial decision layer**:

1. identify revenue opportunities;
2. rank them by expected value/confidence;
3. make the recommended action obvious;
4. reduce execution friction;
5. attribute the result;
6. learn from the result.

This converts CRM/history from passive storage into an operating loop.

## 4. ICP discipline

Follower count is a weak proxy. Prioritize evidence of monetizable behavior.

### Strong ICP signals

- already sells at least one legal product/service/membership;
- receives commercial questions in DMs/comments/WhatsApp/email;
- has repeat customers or plausible repeat purchase;
- personally operates sales or has a tiny team;
- customer information is fragmented;
- cannot confidently answer who should be contacted today;
- audience trust is already established;
- can make an offer without weeks of production;
- founder can reach the creator directly.

### Negative ICP signals

- audience exists but no offer and no willingness to sell;
- business depends almost entirely on brand sponsorships with no direct customer relationship;
- enterprise team already operates sophisticated lifecycle CRM;
- creator expects Mara to create audience demand from zero;
- regulated/unsupported business that creates disproportionate compliance risk.

### Initial vertical exploration

Run narrow parallel discovery rather than declaring a permanent niche too early. Favor creator businesses with high purchase intent and obvious follow-up opportunities: fitness/coaching, education/expertise, beauty/style, specialist services and digital products.

After 10–20 serious conversations, concentrate on the vertical with the strongest combination of pain, speed to value, willingness to act and measurable repeat revenue.

## 5. The enemy is revenue leakage

Marketing becomes stronger when Mara names the problem.

Useful problem language:

- followers who never become identifiable customers;
- buyers who disappear after one purchase;
- high-value customers nobody follows up with;
- DMs with buying intent that get buried;
- creators guessing what to sell next;
- customer history scattered across tools;
- campaigns sent to everyone instead of the right people;
- no credible measurement of what generated the second purchase.

Mara is not fighting another SaaS logo. It is fighting **revenue leakage and commercial guesswork**.

## 6. Offer architecture

### Entry offer: Mara Revenue Audit

A short founder-led diagnosis of the creator's current monetization system.

Deliver:
- map current offers and buying paths;
- identify customer/revenue data available;
- identify 3 concrete leakage points;
- propose the highest-confidence next revenue action;
- estimate what must be measured to prove impact.

Do not promise guaranteed revenue.

### Activation offer: 7-Day Mara Revenue Sprint

Goal: reach the second Mara magic moment as quickly as possible.

1. establish baseline;
2. import/map enough customer context;
3. identify 3–10 opportunities;
4. creator approves one action;
5. execute through the safest available channel;
6. measure response/purchase;
7. produce a before/action/after evidence record.

The sprint is successful even if revenue does not increase when it produces decisive learning about ICP, offer, timing or product gaps. Marketing claims, however, must distinguish learning from proven lift.

## 7. Founder-led acquisition system

Paid ads are premature before message and activation are proven.

Initial channel order:

1. highly personalized founder outbound;
2. warm introductions;
3. creator referrals;
4. public revenue teardowns / educational content;
5. partnerships with creator managers, small agencies and communities;
6. SEO/problem-led content once language stabilizes;
7. paid acquisition only after conversion economics are observable.

### Outbound rule

Never open with a product tour.

A good outbound message demonstrates that we inspected the creator's business and found a plausible commercial problem.

Structure:

`OBSERVATION → POSSIBLE REVENUE LEAK → SMALL CREDIBLE OFFER → LOW-FRICTION CTA`

Example logic (not canonical copy):

"Vi que vendes X y que la mayor parte del tráfico parece venir de Instagram. Estoy trabajando en Mara para detectar compradores que deberían recibir una segunda oferta y medir si esa acción realmente genera recompra. Estoy haciendo este análisis con pocos creadores antes de abrirlo. Si quieres, te muestro 2–3 oportunidades que buscaría en tu negocio."

## 8. Proof before persuasion

The strongest marketing asset is not a polished campaign. It is a credible evidence object.

Evidence hierarchy:

1. attributed incremental purchase/repeat purchase;
2. creator case study with baseline, action and result;
3. creator quote tied to a specific workflow/result;
4. screenshot of a useful detected opportunity;
5. quantified revenue audit;
6. product demo;
7. feature claim;
8. generic brand content.

Move marketing effort upward in this hierarchy.

## 9. Case-study machine

Every pilot should be designed to create reusable learning/proof with permission.

Case-study template:

- creator type and business model;
- starting problem;
- baseline window;
- opportunity Mara identified;
- action creator approved;
- audience/customer segment targeted;
- result window;
- revenue/conversion/repeat-purchase result;
- attribution confidence;
- what would have happened without Mara, when measurable;
- creator quote;
- limitations.

Never turn correlation into fake incremental lift.

## 10. Content strategy

Mara content should teach creators to think like commercial operators without making them feel like corporations.

Content pillars:

### Revenue Leakage
Show where creator businesses lose money after audience acquisition.

### Customer Intelligence
Teach creators to distinguish followers, leads, first-time buyers, repeat-ready buyers, VIPs and inactive high-value customers.

### Next Best Action
Use anonymized/hypothetical examples of what a creator should do today and why.

### Creator Business Teardowns
Analyze public monetization journeys and identify opportunities without pretending to know private revenue.

### Evidence / Build in Public
Publish real learnings, experiments and results when permission/privacy allow.

### Operator Education
AOV, repeat purchase, LTV, conversion, cohorts and retention explained through creator-native examples.

Content CTA should usually be diagnostic: "Want me to map where your next repeat purchase could come from?" rather than "Sign up for our all-in-one platform."

## 11. Message hierarchy

### Level 1 — outcome
More revenue from the audience you already have.

### Level 2 — problem
You know your followers better than you know your customers.

### Level 3 — mechanism
Mara connects purchases and customer history to detect the next best commercial action.

### Level 4 — proof
Show a specific opportunity/action/result.

### Level 5 — category
Creator Revenue OS.

Category language should support comprehension, not replace the outcome.

## 12. Landing page doctrine

Above the fold must answer:

1. Who is this for?
2. What economic outcome does it improve?
3. How is it different?
4. What should I do next?

Candidate hero direction:

**Your followers are on social. Your customers should live in Mara.**

Supporting copy:

**See who buys, who is ready to buy again, and what revenue action to take next.**

CTA during validation:

**Get a Revenue Audit** / **Join the Founding Creator Pilot**

Avoid a giant feature grid above proof.

## 13. Funnel

Track the founder-led funnel explicitly:

`QUALIFIED PROSPECT → CONTACTED → REPLIED → DISCOVERY → AUDIT → PILOT ACCEPTED → ACTIVATED → OPPORTUNITY SHOWN → ACTION EXECUTED → PURCHASE/LEARNING → RETAINED → REFERRED`

Measure conversion between every state.

Early bottleneck diagnosis:

- low reply → targeting/message problem;
- replies but no discovery → credibility/CTA problem;
- discovery but no pilot → pain/value/trust problem;
- pilot but no activation → onboarding/workload problem;
- activation but no opportunity → ICP/data/product problem;
- opportunity but no action → recommendation trust/usability problem;
- action but no purchase → offer/timing/model problem;
- purchase but no retention → recurring value problem.

Do not solve every failure by adding features.

## 14. Metrics

Marketing metrics must connect to revenue validation.

Primary early metrics:
- qualified reply rate;
- discovery booking rate;
- audit → pilot conversion;
- time to activation;
- time to first useful opportunity;
- opportunity execution rate;
- time to first Mara-attributed purchase;
- creator week-2/week-4 return;
- referral rate.

Economic metrics as data matures:
- CAC by channel;
- gross profit per activated creator;
- GMV per active creator;
- Mara revenue per creator;
- payback period;
- creator retention / GMV retention;
- incremental revenue lift with credible attribution.

Vanity follower growth is not a primary success metric.

## 15. Pricing marketing principle

Do not lead with cheapness. Lead with aligned economics and measurable value.

Current thesis supports low/no fixed entry cost plus a success-based take rate, with Pro later. Competitors already make storefront and basic creator tooling inexpensive; therefore price alone cannot be the moat.

Pricing research must test:
- creator resistance to take rate;
- preference for fixed SaaS after meaningful GMV;
- willingness to pay for proven repeat-revenue intelligence;
- threshold at which creators prefer lower take rate + subscription;
- whether payment processing through Mara is acceptable or creators require external rails.

Do not permanently lock 10–15% without evidence.

## 16. Growth loops

### Revenue proof loop
Creator gets result → permits proof → proof improves acquisition → more creators → more results.

### Referral loop
Creator receives useful revenue opportunity → invites another creator → both receive a benefit tied to activation, not vanity signup.

### Intelligence loop
More legitimate commerce events → better commercial patterns → better recommendations → better outcomes → higher retention. Do not claim network effects until evidence exists.

### Customer-powered loop (later)
A successful buyer experience can expose Mara subtly and create creator discovery without turning Mara into a consumer social network.

## 17. 30/60/90 strategic progression

### Days 0–30: learn manually
- 50 deeply researched prospects;
- 15+ meaningful creator conversations target;
- 5 pilots target;
- founder manually supports audits and opportunity detection;
- record objections verbatim;
- seek first attributable second purchase.

### Days 31–60: concentrate
- identify highest-performing ICP cluster;
- rewrite landing/outbound around its language;
- create 2–3 strong evidence assets;
- formalize referral ask;
- reduce time-to-value;
- test pricing with creators who experienced value.

### Days 61–90: find repeatability
- run one dominant outbound motion;
- one content motion;
- one partnership experiment;
- measure CAC/time cost;
- automate only repeated manual work;
- test whether founder-led acquisition can be delegated without destroying conversion.

## 18. Kill criteria and strategic honesty

Mara must be willing to learn that a segment or message is wrong.

Warning signals:
- creators like demos but refuse to connect/import customer data;
- detected opportunities are obvious and add no value;
- creators do not execute recommendations;
- repeat purchase is structurally rare in chosen ICP;
- creators value storefront more than intelligence, making Mara undifferentiated;
- creator economics cannot support Mara's take rate;
- attribution cannot be made credible enough to support the promise.

These are reasons to change ICP, workflow, pricing or positioning—not reasons to manufacture stronger marketing claims.

## 19. Marketing operating principle

# DO NOT MARKET MARA AS SOFTWARE. MARKET THE REVENUE DECISION.

Every campaign, landing page, outreach message and demo should eventually answer:

> What should this creator do next to make more money from the customers/audience they already have, and can Mara prove that the action helped?

That is the marketing standard.
