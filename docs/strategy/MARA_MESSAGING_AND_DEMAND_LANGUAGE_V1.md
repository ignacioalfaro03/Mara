# MARA — MESSAGING AND DEMAND LANGUAGE V1

Status: strategy working doctrine
Date: 2026-09-12
Scope: creator acquisition, positioning, landing-page language, founder outreach, demand discovery

## Strategic premise

Mara should not lead with software, AI, CRM, automation, analytics, storefronts, funnels or payments.

Those are implementation details.

The creator-facing commercial problem is simpler:

> You already have attention. You are losing revenue because you do not know who is ready to buy, what to offer next, or when to act.

Mara should make this pain obvious before it explains the product.

## Core enemy

The enemy is **revenue leakage**.

Revenue leakage means money that plausibly could have been captured from existing audience or customers but was lost because:

- a buyer purchased once and was never contacted again;
- a high-intent DM was forgotten;
- a customer became inactive unnoticed;
- an offer was sent to the wrong people;
- the creator did not know what to sell next;
- the creator delayed follow-up;
- customer context lived in memory, DMs or spreadsheets;
- the creator had traffic but no structured customer lifecycle;
- the creator optimized views instead of buyer behavior;
- everyone received the same message regardless of intent.

This framing is stronger than generic `monetization` because it starts from money the creator believes may already be available.

## Creator language hypothesis

Avoid abstract SaaS language.

Weak language:

- monetize your audience;
- all-in-one creator platform;
- AI-powered CRM;
- grow your creator business;
- supercharge your revenue;
- automate your funnel;
- powerful analytics;
- audience intelligence.

These phrases are crowded, vague, and easy to ignore.

Prefer concrete situations:

- Who bought from you once and is ready to buy again?
- Which customer should you contact today?
- Which DMs are actually purchase intent?
- Who has gone quiet after spending money with you?
- What should you offer your best buyers next?
- Which customers are worth following up with — and which are not?
- How much money are you leaving inside customers you already acquired?
- You know your followers. Do you know your customers?
- Your audience lives on social. Your customers should not disappear inside social.

## Primary positioning line

Working default:

> **Turn followers into customers. Know who to sell to next.**

Supporting line:

> Mara finds the customers, follow-ups and offers most likely to produce your next sale — using the audience and customers you already have.

## Strong alternative positioning territories

### Territory A — Stop guessing

> **Stop guessing what to sell next.**

> Mara tells you who deserves your attention, what they may want next, and what action to take.

Use when the creator already sells but operates reactively.

### Territory B — Revenue leakage

> **You may already have your next sales. You just cannot see them.**

> Mara finds revenue opportunities hiding inside previous buyers, conversations and customer behavior.

Use for acquisition and audits.

### Territory C — Followers vs customers

> **Followers are rented. Customers are your business.**

> Mara helps you turn social attention into customer relationships you can understand and grow.

Use carefully. Do not overclaim ownership where platform/data constraints apply.

### Territory D — Second purchase

> **The first sale is not the business. The second sale is.**

> Mara helps creators turn one-time buyers into repeat customers.

Strong differentiation candidate because competitors often lead on first-sale infrastructure.

## Recommended initial wedge message

For creators already making some sales:

> You probably do not need more followers first.
>
> You need to know which people already in your audience or customer base are most likely to buy next.
>
> Mara helps you find them, decide what to offer, and measure whether the action actually made money.

## Emotional conflict to solve

Creators often face a tension:

`I want to earn more` vs `I do not want my audience to feel exploited.`

Mara should resolve the tension by positioning **relevance over pressure**.

Do not say:

> Sell more to everyone.

Say:

> Know who actually has a reason to hear from you.

Do not say:

> Automate more messages.

Say:

> Stop bothering people who are unlikely to care.

Do not say:

> Extract more revenue from fans.

Say:

> Make more relevant offers to customers who have already shown intent.

This is commercially strong and better aligned with trust preservation.

## Anti-spam positioning

A potentially strong Mara contrast:

> **Better targeting, fewer unnecessary messages.**

If Mara works, it should reduce indiscriminate selling.

Possible message:

> Mara does not tell you to message everyone. It tells you who is worth contacting — and when doing nothing is the better action.

This should become a product and marketing principle.

## Category narrative

### Old creator stack

`POST → GET VIEWS → HOPE → LINK IN BIO → FIRST SALE`

### Mara stack

`AUDIENCE → BUYER → CUSTOMER MEMORY → OPPORTUNITY → RELEVANT ACTION → SECOND PURCHASE`

The narrative is not that existing tools are bad.

The narrative is:

> Creator platforms became good at helping you publish and sell something. The next problem is knowing what to do after someone buys.

That is the category opening.

## Founder outreach doctrine

Never cold-DM:

> Hey, I built a SaaS for creators. Want to try it?

The outreach itself should provide value.

### Pattern 1 — Revenue leak observation

> I was looking at how you sell [specific offer]. I noticed one thing I think could be leaving money on the table: [specific observation]. I am building Mara around exactly this problem — helping creators find the next sale inside people who already showed intent. If useful, I can send you the 3 opportunities I would test first. No pitch required.

### Pattern 2 — Buyer journey teardown

> I went through your buying flow like a customer. The first-sale experience is clear, but I could not see what happens after someone buys. I am researching repeat revenue for creators and wrote down a few ideas for your business. Want me to send them?

### Pattern 3 — Diagnostic question

> Quick question because I am studying this deeply: when someone buys from you once, do you have a real system for knowing when or what to sell them next, or is it mostly manual today?

### Pattern 4 — No-more-followers frame

> You already seem to have enough audience to learn something interesting. I am more curious about what happens to your existing buyers than how to get you more followers. I am building Mara around that exact gap.

Rule: every outreach message must prove that the creator was actually inspected.

## Revenue Audit offer

The best acquisition offer should be diagnostic before it is software.

### Name

**Mara Revenue Audit**

### Promise

> We look at how your audience becomes customers, what happens after purchase, and where repeat revenue may be leaking.

### Deliverable

Creator receives:

1. buyer-path map;
2. top revenue leaks;
3. 3 concrete actions to test;
4. one recommended next offer/follow-up;
5. measurement plan.

No account creation should be required before value is demonstrated where operationally feasible.

## 7-Day Revenue Sprint

The audit should naturally lead into:

> Let Mara help execute one of these opportunities for seven days and see whether it produces money.

Success condition is not product usage.

Success is one of:

- repeat purchase;
- recovered checkout;
- conversion from a high-intent lead;
- reactivation purchase;
- increased AOV from a relevant upsell;
- measurable revenue opportunity accepted and acted on.

## Hooks to test

### Direct pain hooks

- You probably do not need more followers.
- Your next customer may already follow you.
- Your next sale may already be in your DMs.
- You got the first sale. What happens next?
- How many customers bought once and disappeared?
- Stop treating every follower like the same person.
- A creator with 20,000 followers can still have zero customer memory.
- More views are not the same as more customers.
- The most expensive customer may be the one you already paid to acquire and forgot.
- Your link in bio knows what you sell. Does it know who should buy next?

### Curiosity hooks

- I think creator businesses are losing money in a place nobody talks about.
- Creator software solved checkout. It has not solved the next sale.
- I studied what happens after a creator gets a buyer. It is surprisingly broken.
- Most creator dashboards answer what happened. Very few answer what you should do today.
- The creator economy has an attribution problem disguised as a growth problem.

### Anti-hype hooks

- You do not need an AI clone.
- You do not need another dashboard.
- You do not need to post more just because revenue is flat.
- You do not need to automate 1,000 DMs.
- You need to know which 10 people actually deserve attention.

Use anti-hype carefully; never imply capabilities Mara has not proven.

## Objection map

### `I already use Stan / Beacons / Patreon / Shopify`

Response strategy:

> Good. Mara does not need to replace the place where you sell first. The question is whether your current stack tells you who should buy next and what action is most likely to create the second purchase.

Long-term integrations may be required; do not promise integrations before they exist.

### `I do not want to spam my audience`

> Neither do we. Mara should help you contact fewer people with more relevant reasons, not blast everyone.

### `I am too small`

> Follower count is not the first filter. Existing purchase intent and a real offer matter more.

### `I do not have time for another platform`

> Mara must earn its place by reducing decisions and showing the next high-value action, not by creating more admin work.

### `AI can already tell me what to sell`

> Generic AI does not automatically have your verified customer history, purchase behavior, offer context and revenue attribution. Mara's value is the commercial context plus the measured action loop, not text generation.

### `I know my customers personally`

> That is an advantage at small scale. Mara becomes useful when memory stops scaling or when you want to know whether intuition is actually converting.

### `Why should I give you my customer data?`

This is a trust-critical objection. Answer with product reality only: creator-scoped access, purpose limitation, clear consent/legal basis where required, deletion/export controls, and no hidden vulnerability profiling. Privacy claims must never outrun implementation.

## Content strategy

Founder content should not mostly announce Mara.

It should repeatedly teach creators to see their business differently.

### Pillar 1 — Audience vs customer

Teach that followers, viewers, leads, first-time buyers and repeat customers are economically different.

### Pillar 2 — Revenue leaks

Break down concrete missed-revenue situations.

### Pillar 3 — The second purchase

Show why repeat behavior matters more than endlessly reacquiring attention.

### Pillar 4 — Commercial systems without becoming salesy

Show that relevance can reduce spam and protect trust.

### Pillar 5 — Creator business teardowns

Publicly analyze opt-in/public sales journeys without inventing private metrics.

### Pillar 6 — Build evidence in public

Share learnings such as:

- what creators actually responded to;
- which opportunity types led to action;
- where attribution failed;
- what Mara got wrong;
- quantified case studies when permission exists.

## Proof hierarchy

Strongest to weakest:

1. verified incremental/repeat purchase tied to Mara action;
2. controlled or credible revenue-lift evidence;
3. creator case study with baseline and result;
4. repeat use of recommendations;
5. specific creator testimonial about a commercial decision;
6. opportunity screenshot with context;
7. audit example;
8. product demo;
9. feature description;
10. generic claim.

Marketing should move upward in this hierarchy over time.

## Language research protocol

Do not invent the final copy in a conference room.

For every creator conversation, capture exact phrases for:

- how they describe selling;
- what they call customers/fans;
- how they describe follow-up;
- what they fear about monetization;
- what feels manual;
- what they forget;
- what they wish they knew;
- what they currently check before making an offer;
- what makes them feel like they are spamming;
- what would make them trust a recommendation.

Tag phrases by:

`PAIN | DESIRE | FEAR | CURRENT_WORKAROUND | TRIGGER | OBJECTION | TRUST | VALUE_LANGUAGE`

Every 10 interviews, rewrite the message bank using observed language.

## Message-market-fit scorecard

For each message test, record:

- target niche;
- creator size/revenue proxy;
- message variant;
- sent;
- opened if observable;
- replied;
- positive reply;
- accepted audit;
- accepted sprint;
- activated;
- executed recommendation;
- generated transaction;
- referral.

Do not optimize only for reply rate. A provocative message can get replies without producing customers.

Primary message success should eventually be:

`message → qualified creator → executed revenue action → evidence`

## Initial message tests

Run at least these conceptual variants before locking positioning:

1. **Revenue leakage** — `I think there is money being left inside existing buyers.`
2. **Second purchase** — `What happens after someone buys once?`
3. **Stop guessing** — `How do you decide who to contact and what to sell next?`
4. **No more followers required** — `You may already have enough audience; the gap may be conversion/retention.`
5. **Anti-spam relevance** — `Contact fewer people, but with better reasons.`

Use comparable prospect groups. Do not declare a winner from tiny samples.

## Negative language rules

Do not use unless directly supported by evidence:

- guaranteed revenue;
- make money automatically;
- passive income machine;
- 10x your revenue;
- AI knows exactly what your fans want;
- never lose a sale again;
- fully automated business;
- replace your sales team;
- own your audience, if underlying data portability/control is not literally true;
- incremental revenue attribution unless methodology supports it.

Mara should sound commercially ambitious but empirically disciplined.

## Brand voice

Mara should sound like a sharp operator, not a creator guru.

Characteristics:

- specific;
- commercially literate;
- calm;
- anti-hype;
- evidence-first;
- respectful of audience trust;
- action-oriented;
- simple language above complex intelligence.

Avoid:

- hustle clichés;
- fake urgency;
- guru income screenshots without attribution;
- excessive AI language;
- manipulation framing;
- fan extraction language;
- infantilizing creators.

## North-star marketing sentence

The strongest current synthesis is:

> **Mara helps creators find their next sale inside the audience and customers they already have — without guessing who to contact, what to offer, or when to act.**

This is a working hypothesis, not sacred copy.

The final language must be earned through creator conversations and revenue evidence.

## Strategic standard

Marketing is not successful when creators understand what Mara does.

Marketing is successful when the right creator immediately recognizes a painful commercial situation from their own business and wants Mara to inspect it.

The desired first reaction is not:

> Interesting software.

It is:

> **That is exactly what happens to me.**
