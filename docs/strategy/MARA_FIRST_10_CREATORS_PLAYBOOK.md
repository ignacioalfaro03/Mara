# MARA — FIRST 10 CREATORS PLAYBOOK

Status: operating playbook  
Effective: 2026-09-12  
Depends on: `MARA_FOUNDATIONAL_THESIS.md` and `MARA_GO_TO_MARKET_V1.md`

## Objective

Recruit and operate the first 10 qualified Mara creators in a way that produces evidence about:

- who values Mara most;
- which revenue opportunities are actionable;
- what creators will pay for;
- what language converts;
- what workflows should be automated;
- whether Mara can generate measurable incremental revenue.

The objective is not 10 signups.

The objective is 10 commercially informative pilots.

## 1. Prospect score

Score each candidate 0–2 on six dimensions.

### A. Existing monetization
0 = no paid offer  
1 = occasional monetization  
2 = active offer with recent buyers

### B. Repeat-purchase potential
0 = mostly one-off  
1 = possible repeat/upsell  
2 = naturally recurring/repeatable

### C. Audience signal
0 = weak/unclear engagement  
1 = some engaged audience  
2 = clear purchase intent/comments/DM behavior

### D. CRM pain
0 = already sophisticated  
1 = mixed/manual systems  
2 = customers mainly in memory, chats or spreadsheets

### E. Accessibility
0 = hard to reach  
1 = reachable through one channel  
2 = realistic direct founder access

### F. Measurement
0 = impossible to attribute  
1 = partial measurement possible  
2 = creator can provide clear sales/action outcome

Prioritize candidates scoring 9+ out of 12.

## 2. Prospect research card

Before contacting a creator, record:

- creator name/handle;
- niche;
- audience size;
- main platform;
- what they sell;
- visible price if available;
- buying path;
- evidence of purchase intent;
- potential repeat opportunity;
- current tools if visible;
- personalized observation;
- reason Mara might help.

No generic outreach before completing this card.

## 3. Outreach principles

Good outreach:

- proves we looked at their actual business;
- speaks about revenue leakage, not software features;
- asks for discovery/validation, not a purchase;
- avoids hype;
- avoids "make money easily" language;
- gives the creator a low-risk reason to answer.

Bad outreach:

- "We built an AI platform for creators";
- "Grow your business with automation";
- mass-copy messages;
- pretending Mara already has proof it does not yet have;
- requesting sensitive data before trust exists.

## 4. Initial outreach sequence

### Touch 1 — observation + question

Structure:

1. specific observation about their offer/business;
2. identify the likely commercial problem;
3. explain Mara in one line;
4. ask whether the problem exists for them.

Example framework:

> Vi que vendes [offer] y gran parte del movimiento parece venir desde [channel]. Estoy trabajando en Mara, una herramienta para creadores que ya venden y quieren entender mejor quién les compra, quién podría volver a comprar y qué oportunidad conviene trabajar después. No te quiero vender nada todavía; estoy validando el problema con creadores reales. ¿Hoy cómo llevas el seguimiento de las personas que ya te compraron?

Do not copy blindly. Personalize the first sentence.

### Touch 2 — value artifact

If no reply after a reasonable interval, send one useful observation, not "bump".

Example:

> Te escribo una última vez porque mirando tu oferta se me ocurrió una hipótesis concreta: [specific repeat/upsell idea]. Ese tipo de oportunidades es justamente lo que quiero que Mara detecte automáticamente. Si te hace sentido, feliz te muestro el concepto en 10 minutos.

### Stop rule

No repeated pressure after two high-quality attempts unless the creator re-engages.

## 5. Discovery call

Duration: 15–25 minutes.

Goal: understand current revenue operations.

Questions:

1. ¿Qué vendes hoy y cuál es lo que más mueve ingresos?
2. ¿Cómo llega normalmente una persona desde seguidor a comprador?
3. ¿Dónde guardas quién te compró?
4. ¿Sabes cuántas personas te compraron más de una vez?
5. ¿Cómo decides a quién volver a contactar?
6. ¿Qué haces después de una primera compra?
7. ¿Qué ventas sientes que se te pierden por falta de seguimiento?
8. ¿Qué herramientas usas hoy?
9. ¿Qué parte de vender te consume más tiempo?
10. Si Mara te dijera "estas 5 personas tienen la mejor oportunidad de compra esta semana", ¿qué necesitarías ver para confiar en eso?
11. ¿Cómo medirías que Mara realmente te ayudó?
12. ¿Qué tendría que pasar en siete días para que quisieras seguir usándolo?

Do not pitch continuously during discovery.

## 6. Qualification decision

After call, choose:

### PILOT NOW
Clear offer + recent customer signal + measurable opportunity.

### NURTURE
Good fit but wrong timing or insufficient data.

### REJECT FOR NOW
No monetization, no repeat potential, no measurement or incompatible business.

Record why.

## 7. Revenue Sprint workflow

### Baseline

Capture:

- offers;
- customers available;
- recent sales period;
- average ticket if known;
- repeat buyers if known;
- current communication flow.

### Opportunity generation

Generate only a few high-confidence actions.

Examples:

- buyer due for replenishment/repeat;
- past high-value buyer inactive;
- customer bought entry product but not logical next product;
- lead asked twice but did not purchase;
- existing buyer suitable for upgrade;
- product buyer suitable for membership/recurring offer.

Every opportunity should contain:

- customer/segment;
- evidence;
- recommended action;
- proposed offer;
- why now;
- expected value hypothesis;
- creator approval status;
- result.

### Action

Creator must approve outbound messaging/offers.

For early pilots, manual execution is acceptable.

### Measurement

Record:

- action sent;
- channel;
- timestamp;
- response;
- checkout created;
- sale/no sale;
- revenue;
- discount/cost if applicable;
- creator time spent;
- whether creator believes the sale would have happened without Mara.

## 8. Success criteria per pilot

Minimum useful pilot outcome is one of:

A. measurable incremental sale;  
B. creator executes a recommendation and confirms material workflow value;  
C. strong falsification signal explaining why the recommendation model is not useful.

Best outcome:

> measurable second purchase or upsell plausibly attributable to a Mara-originated opportunity.

## 9. End-of-pilot interview

Ask:

1. ¿Qué fue lo más útil?
2. ¿Qué recomendación no te hizo sentido?
3. ¿Hubo algo que Mara te mostró que no habrías visto solo?
4. ¿Te hizo ganar dinero o ahorrar tiempo de forma concreta?
5. ¿Seguirías usando esto mañana?
6. ¿Pagarías por ello? ¿Cómo te haría más sentido: porcentaje de ventas, mensualidad o combinación?
7. ¿Qué parte te generó desconfianza?
8. ¿A quién conoces que tenga este mismo problema?

## 10. Evidence scorecard

For each creator:

- qualified: yes/no;
- activated: yes/no;
- first offer live: yes/no;
- customer data available: yes/no;
- opportunity generated: yes/no;
- recommendation acted on: yes/no;
- incremental sale: yes/no;
- incremental GMV;
- creator time saved estimate;
- usefulness score 1–10;
- willingness to continue 1–10;
- willingness to pay: yes/no/maybe;
- preferred pricing model;
- referral given: yes/no;
- key objection;
- key quote;
- next product implication.

## 11. First 10 target mix

Do not fill all ten with random creators.

Suggested mix:

1. fitness coach with 1:1 / recurring programs;
2. trainer selling plans or sessions;
3. beauty creator with consultations/products;
4. styling/fashion creator with paid advisory/digital products;
5. educator selling course/session;
6. niche expert with paid consults;
7. visual creator selling presets/templates/assets;
8. creator selling membership/community access;
9. lifestyle creator with affiliate + owned paid offer;
10. strongest opportunistic candidate discovered during outreach.

After creator #5, reassess the mix. If one niche is clearly stronger, concentrate creators #6–10 there.

## 12. Weekly founder cadence

### Monday
Build/review prospect list. Research 10 candidates.

### Tuesday
Send 5–10 high-quality personalized outreach messages.

### Wednesday
Discovery calls + pilot onboarding.

### Thursday
Operate opportunities + capture friction.

### Friday
Review outcomes and update:

- best message;
- strongest ICP;
- objections;
- product gaps;
- revenue results;
- next week's targeting.

## 13. Founder content cadence

Initially 2–3 useful pieces/week is enough.

Content should come from real operating observations.

Formats:

- short teardown;
- "3 places creators lose repeat sales";
- anonymized pilot insight;
- mini case study;
- screenshot/demo of Today/Opportunity;
- founder note about creator revenue operations.

Do not flood channels with generic AI-generated content.

## 14. Case study template

Title:

> How [creator type] found $X in repeat revenue with Mara

Sections:

1. Business context
2. Existing workflow
3. Missed opportunity
4. Mara recommendation
5. Creator action
6. Result
7. Time to result
8. Creator quote
9. What Mara learned

Use exact numbers only with permission and evidence.

## 15. Kill / pivot signals

Question the current wedge if after 10 qualified pilots:

- creators do not act on recommendations;
- opportunities are obvious and provide no incremental insight;
- attribution is impossible;
- repeat purchase potential is too weak;
- creator economics cannot support Mara's take rate;
- acquisition requires disproportionate founder effort with weak retention.

Do not hide these signals by optimizing signup volume.

## 16. Scale signal

Begin scaling acquisition only when a repeatable pattern appears:

> Similar creators repeatedly activate, act on Mara opportunities, generate measurable value, remain engaged, and refer peers.

At that point, specialize messaging around the strongest creator segment and move from founder-led discovery into repeatable channel acquisition.
