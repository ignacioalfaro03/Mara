# MARA — VISUAL IDENTITY & INSTAGRAM ART DIRECTION V1

Status: STRATEGIC DIRECTION — DRAFT
Effective: 2026-09-12
Authority: subordinate to `MARA_FOUNDATIONAL_THESIS.md`

## 0. Purpose

Define a visual system that makes Mara recognizable before the viewer reads the account name.

Mara must not look like:
- a generic SaaS startup;
- an AI tool;
- a crypto product;
- a motivational creator account;
- a Canva carousel factory;
- a growth-hacking page;
- a glossy agency deck;
- stock-photo creator economy content.

Mara should look like:

> **a serious editorial publication about the business behind creators — with access to the evidence, numbers and commercial decisions that others do not see.**

Core aesthetic territory:

# CREATOR BUSINESS EDITORIAL

Reference feeling, not literal imitation:
- financial newspaper clarity;
- fashion/editorial photography restraint;
- field-notebook evidence;
- documentary realism;
- modern product intelligence;
- creator culture without influencer clichés.

The system must feel human, commercial, precise and alive.

---

# 1. The visual idea

## 1.1 Brand tension

Mara sits between two worlds:

**CREATOR CULTURE**
- personality;
- spontaneity;
- texture;
- human imperfection;
- phone-native reality;
- emotion;
- momentum.

and

**COMMERCIAL INTELLIGENCE**
- numbers;
- evidence;
- prioritization;
- precision;
- decisions;
- accountability;
- revenue.

Most brands choose one side.

Mara should visually combine both.

That combination is the distinctive territory.

## 1.2 Visual promise

Every piece should feel like:

> **We looked at the business underneath the content.**

Not:

> We made a marketing post about creators.

# 2. Distinctive Brand Assets

Mara needs a small set of repeated assets used with discipline.

## DBA-01 — The Revenue Redline

A single thin editorial line used to:
- underline the commercial truth;
- connect a customer to an action;
- strike through wasted effort;
- mark where revenue leaks;
- annotate evidence.

Behavior:
- straight or slightly hand-drawn;
- never decorative;
- always points to meaning;
- should become a Mara signature.

Example:

`FIRST SALE ───────── SECOND SALE`

or

`Bought 42 days ago  ──→  likely repeat window`

The line is more important than a logo stamp.

## DBA-02 — The Mara Margin Note

Short side annotations in a narrow margin.

Examples:
- `MARA NOTE 01`
- `REVENUE LEAK`
- `WHY THIS MATTERS`
- `NEXT ACTION`
- `PROOF`

Use as if an analyst/editor physically marked the page.

The margin note gives Mara an editorial intelligence signature.

## DBA-03 — Commercial Highlight Box

A restrained rectangular highlight used only for the decision.

Inside it:
- person/customer;
- opportunity;
- recommended action;
- expected/observed outcome.

Never use it as a generic UI card decoration.

## DBA-04 — Evidence Stamp

Small labels for factual status:
- `OBSERVED`
- `ATTRIBUTED`
- `TESTING`
- `HYPOTHESIS`
- `CREATOR QUOTE`
- `REAL ORDER`
- `REPEAT BUYER`

This reinforces Mara's evidence-first philosophy and makes posts visually identifiable.

## DBA-05 — The Second Sale Marker

Whenever relevant, use a simple repeated marker:

`01 → 02`

Meaning:
- first purchase;
- second purchase;
- relationship becomes commercially meaningful.

This can become one of Mara's strongest recognizable motifs.

Do not over-brand it. Keep it simple.

## DBA-06 — Revenue Number

One number can dominate a frame when it is real and meaningful.

Examples:
- `$38.900`
- `42 DAYS`
- `3 BUYERS`
- `1 REPEAT PURCHASE`
- `17%`

Numbers should feel like editorial evidence, not performance-marketing clickbait.

# 3. Color direction

## 3.1 Philosophy

Do not build recognition through a rainbow palette.

The system should be mostly neutral so that:
- photographs carry life;
- evidence carries attention;
- one accent becomes highly distinctive.

## 3.2 Working palette architecture

### Base
- paper/off-white background;
- near-black type;
- muted warm gray;
- occasional true white.

### Accent
One proprietary Mara accent should be selected after visual testing.

Recommended territory:
- deep oxblood / wine red;
- not bright SaaS red;
- not neon;
- not pink;
- not purple.

Reason:
- commercial seriousness;
- editorial association;
- contrasts with creator-tech category dominated by blue/purple gradients;
- works naturally with annotation/redline metaphor.

Working role:
**MARA RED = decision / evidence / revenue signal.**

Not a decorative fill color.

## 3.3 Usage ratio

Approximate visual ratio:
- 70–80% neutral;
- 15–25% real photography / source material;
- <10% Mara accent.

If the accent dominates the post, the system is probably being over-designed.

# 4. Typography

## 4.1 Typography must feel editorial, not startup

Avoid:
- bubbly rounded SaaS fonts;
- futuristic tech fonts;
- monospace everywhere;
- fake handwritten fonts;
- excessive bold condensed headline culture.

## 4.2 Type roles

### Display Serif
Purpose:
- strong editorial statements;
- selected covers;
- big conceptual ideas;
- case-study titles.

Feel:
- contemporary editorial serif;
- sharp, intelligent, not luxury-fashion theatrical.

### Grotesk / Sans
Purpose:
- body text;
- captions;
- charts;
- data;
- utility labels;
- interface screenshots.

Feel:
- neutral;
- highly legible;
- not trendy for trend's sake.

### Mono — restricted
Use only for:
- transaction IDs;
- timestamps;
- raw evidence;
- small data labels;
- system events.

Never make mono the primary brand typeface.

## 4.3 Hierarchy

Posts should usually have only 3 levels:
1. statement;
2. evidence/context;
3. annotation.

Avoid seven text sizes in one carousel.

## 4.4 Text casing

Preferred:
- sentence case for almost everything;
- uppercase only for evidence stamps, tiny labels and occasional hard editorial emphasis.

This keeps Mara intelligent rather than shouty.

---

# 5. Layout grammar

## 5.1 Grid

Use a consistent editorial grid:
- generous outer margins;
- one dominant content zone;
- one narrow annotation margin;
- intentional negative space.

The page should feel composed, not decorated.

## 5.2 Alignment

Default:
- left aligned;
- optical rather than perfectly mathematical when photography demands it.

Avoid excessive center alignment.

## 5.3 White space

White space is a brand asset.

Do not fill every carousel slide.

If a slide has one sentence worth reading, allow it to have one sentence.

## 5.4 Borders

Thin editorial rules only.

No:
- rounded floating cards everywhere;
- gradient outlines;
- glowing UI containers;
- oversized drop shadows.

## 5.5 Corners

Photography: natural square/rectangular crops.

UI screenshots: retain product-native corners if needed.

Do not impose a universal rounded-corner SaaS aesthetic on everything.

# 6. Photography doctrine

## 6.1 The subject

Photograph the business behind the creator.

Not generic lifestyle.

Subjects:
- creator packing an order;
- phone with DMs open;
- handwritten customer notes;
- creator editing a product page;
- real workspace at night;
- tripod half-folded after shooting;
- product inventory;
- packaging materials;
- order notification;
- creator doing a call;
- notebook with pricing ideas;
- customer list on screen;
- creator walking between shoots/jobs;
- café table with laptop and receipts;
- actual product creation;
- fulfillment mess;
- creator checking revenue between normal life moments.

## 6.2 Photograph the in-between

The best Mara photograph is often not the polished final shoot.

It is:
- before;
- after;
- between;
- backstage;
- the invisible commercial work.

This is where Mara's thesis lives.

## 6.3 Camera feel

Preferred:
- iPhone / high-quality phone;
- documentary digital camera;
- occasional direct flash;
- natural available light;
- slight motion;
- imperfect framing allowed;
- real textures.

Avoid:
- AI people;
- hyper-retouched faces;
- stock lighting;
- fake offices;
- fake luxury;
- sterile 3D sets.

## 6.4 Portrait rule

When photographing creators:
- do not make every image a smiling founder portrait;
- show concentration, movement, decisions and work;
- crop hands, screens, workspace, gestures;
- let identity emerge through reality rather than posing.

## 6.5 Image integrity

Never fake commercial proof visually.

If a DM/order/value is reconstructed for privacy:
- label it as reconstructed;
- preserve the commercial logic;
- never imply a synthetic mockup is a real transaction.

# 7. Screenshot and evidence treatment

Screenshots are a major Mara medium.

But raw screenshots alone look cheap.

## 7.1 Rules

- crop tightly around the commercial event;
- redact names/data when needed;
- keep native UI recognizable;
- add one or two Mara annotations maximum;
- use redline/highlight only where attention should go;
- add evidence stamp when status matters.

## 7.2 Good screenshot post

Example:

Frame 1:
`She bought once. Then nothing happened.`

Frame 2:
real/redacted order record.

Frame 3:
42-day gap annotated.

Frame 4:
Mara opportunity recommendation.

Frame 5:
creator sends action.

Frame 6:
observed result.

That is a story.

Not a product screenshot dump.

# 8. Carousel system

Mara carousels should be mini-investigations.

## 8.1 Default 6-slide structure

1. **Recognition** — the creator sees their problem.
2. **Evidence** — show what actually happens.
3. **Mechanism** — explain why.
4. **Cost** — what revenue/opportunity is lost.
5. **Decision** — what should happen next.
6. **Mara thesis / CTA** — subtle, specific.

## 8.2 Cover rules

A Mara cover should usually be one of:
- a hard statement;
- a real photograph + one line;
- a real number;
- a screenshot/evidence crop;
- a question with commercial tension.

Avoid:
- icon clouds;
- multiple illustrations;
- generic 3D art;
- huge logo;
- “5 tips to…” unless the insight is genuinely specific.

## 8.3 Cover examples

- `She bought once. Then you lost her.`
- `42 days since her last purchase.`
- `Your best customer may not be your biggest follower.`
- `This DM was worth more than 10,000 views.`
- `The first sale is not the business.`
- `You do not have a follower problem.`

# 9. Reel art direction

Reels should feel documented, not generated.

## 9.1 Primary formats

### Format A — Field Note
10–25 seconds.
Real footage.
One commercial observation.
Minimal voiceover or on-screen text.

Example:
creator packing three orders.
Text:
`Three customers bought this week.`
cut.
`Which one is most likely to buy again?`

### Format B — Revenue Autopsy
20–45 seconds.
Screen + physical context.
Annotate where a business is leaking revenue.

### Format C — Creator Business Portrait
30–60 seconds.
Real creator speaking about one business problem, not life story.

### Format D — Mara Found It
Show the actual chain:
data → opportunity → action → outcome.

## 9.2 Motion language

- hard cuts;
- purposeful zoom/crop;
- subtle type entrance;
- no floating 3D elements;
- no fake typing animation;
- no AI voice;
- no overused “cinematic startup” transitions.

Sound:
- real room sound when useful;
- creator voice;
- restrained music;
- no compulsory trending audio if it weakens tone.

# 10. Stories

Stories are Mara's field notebook.

Feed = durable editorial memory.
Stories = live observation.

Story content:
- poll about a real commercial problem;
- screenshot of an observation;
- “we noticed this today”;
- creator audit snippets;
- behind-the-scenes research;
- actual product decisions;
- audience questions;
- mini case updates;
- launch proof.

Do not make Stories a sequence of polished marketing templates.

Use lower-production documentation deliberately.

# 11. Editorial franchises — visual differentiation

Each recurring franchise should be identifiable without changing the whole brand.

## 11.1 YOU ARE LOSING THIS SALE
Visual cue:
- full-bleed real-life image or screenshot;
- redline pointing to the leak;
- hard one-line cover.

## 11.2 THE SECOND SALE
Visual cue:
- clean neutral background;
- `01 → 02` marker;
- editorial serif statement;
- more conceptual and iconic.

## 11.3 REVENUE AUTOPSY
Visual cue:
- evidence-heavy;
- screenshot/table/order sequence;
- margin notes;
- numbered observations.

## 11.4 CREATOR BUSINESS NOTES
Visual cue:
- photography first;
- handwritten/field-note feeling via layout, not fake handwriting;
- short observations.

## 11.5 MARA FOUND IT
Visual cue:
- restrained product proof;
- Commercial Highlight Box;
- `NEXT ACTION` annotation;
- outcome if available.

## 11.6 NO, YOU DO NOT NEED TO…
Visual cue:
- stark typographic post;
- plenty of white space;
- one crossed-out assumption using Revenue Redline.

# 12. Feed composition

Do not design the feed as a checkerboard puzzle.

Each post must work independently.

But a 3x3 view should show healthy rhythm:
- photo;
- typography;
- evidence;
- portrait;
- screenshot;
- conceptual editorial.

Avoid 9 identical carousel covers in a row.

Working rhythm across 9 posts:
1. documentary photo;
2. hard typography;
3. evidence carousel;
4. creator portrait;
5. Second Sale conceptual;
6. screenshot/revenue leak;
7. field note reel;
8. Revenue Autopsy;
9. Mara Found It proof.

The feed should feel like a magazine issue assembled over time.

# 13. Logo discipline

The logo is not the content.

Rules:
- no large logo on every post;
- logo optional on feed assets when account context is enough;
- if used, small and quiet;
- recognition should come from system assets first.

Goal:

If removing the logo makes the post anonymous, the visual system is not distinctive enough.

---

# 14. Caption voice

Captions should read like a smart operator wrote them after observing something real.

Voice:
- precise;
- commercially literate;
- curious;
- never guru-like;
- never hypey;
- never “10x your creator business”.

Structure:
1. observation;
2. implication;
3. decision;
4. optional question/CTA.

Example:

`A creator can spend months finding a buyer and then treat that buyer like a stranger 30 days later.`

`The problem is not only acquisition. The commercial memory disappears after checkout.`

`That is the gap Mara is built around.`

CTA:
`If you sell through Instagram and this happens to you, send us “second sale”.`

# 15. Anti-AI-cheap-content doctrine

The issue is not that AI tools exist.

The issue is visual and intellectual cheapness.

Forbidden:
- generated “creator working on laptop” images;
- fake screenshots;
- generic ChatGPT-written quote posts;
- motivational filler;
- listicles with no original evidence;
- generated faces as case-study subjects;
- prompt-art pretending to be documentary work;
- pseudo-insight because a calendar needs content;
- repetitive template carousels with different copy;
- stock video with captions;
- fake UGC.

Permitted use of AI behind the scenes:
- research assistance;
- transcript cleanup;
- editing support;
- idea clustering;
- analysis;
- production assistance.

But the published artifact should contain a real observation, real person, real evidence, real analysis or genuinely original point of view.

Rule:

# AI MAY ASSIST THE PROCESS. IT MUST NOT REPLACE THE SOURCE MATERIAL.

---

# 16. Quality gate before publishing

A post does not ship unless it passes at least 5/7:

1. **Recognition** — will a creator think “this is me”?
2. **Specificity** — is this about a real commercial situation?
3. **Originality** — did Mara add a genuine observation/analysis?
4. **Evidence** — is there proof, example or mechanism?
5. **Distinctiveness** — could this visually be recognized as Mara?
6. **Utility** — does it change a decision?
7. **Commercial relevance** — does it connect to revenue, customer memory, offer, conversion, retention or workload?

Automatic fail if:
- generic AI visual;
- generic motivational quote;
- empty trend participation;
- copied creator-economy advice;
- vanity announcement with no relevance.

# 17. Working visual manifesto

Mara should feel:
- observed, not generated;
- edited, not templated;
- intelligent, not technical;
- commercial, not corporate;
- human, not soft;
- premium, not luxurious;
- contemporary, not trendy;
- distinctive, not loud.

The strongest Mara post should make someone think:

> `They understand how creator businesses actually work.`

before they think:

> `This is software.`
