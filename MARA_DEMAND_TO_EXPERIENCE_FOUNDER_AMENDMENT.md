# MARA — DEMAND-TO-EXPERIENCE FOUNDER AMENDMENT V1

Status: **HIGHEST-PRIORITY STRATEGIC EXTENSION**  
Effective: **2026-09-08**  
Authority: Founder direction. When this amendment conflicts with older creator-only, storefront-only, chatbot-first, content-first or Mara-Vera-only framing, this amendment wins.

> **MARA TURNS DESIRE INTO DEMAND, AND DEMAND INTO EXPERIENCES.**

---

## 0. WHAT CHANGED

Mara is no longer defined only by the supply side of the creator economy.

The company-level opportunity is broader:

> **Mara is a demand-to-experience marketplace that lets people express what they want to happen, lets others join that demand, validates willingness to pay, and lets Hosts / Creators / Operators turn validated demand into real digital, physical or hybrid experiences.**

The private creator economy remains a strategic supply engine inside Mara. It is not discarded.

Mara Vera remains **Creator Zero** and the first demand-generating universe. She is not the ceiling of the marketplace.

---

## 1. COMPANY LOOP

Canonical marketplace loop:

`DESIRE → DISCOVERY → JOIN → COMMIT → VERIFIED DEMAND → HOST / CREATOR / OPERATOR → UNLOCK → TRANSACTION → EXPERIENCE → MEMORY → NEXT DESIRE`

The product must make three actions obvious:

1. **See what people want to happen.**
2. **Join an existing demand.**
3. **Propose something new.**

The user is not required to think like an event producer. A raw request such as “quiero karaoke con gente de Mara en Chillán” is a valid starting point.

---

## 2. THE PRIMARY ASSET: VERIFIED DEMAND

Likes are weak signals.

The strategic asset is aggregated, privacy-safe, economically meaningful demand.

Mara must distinguish:

- interest;
- intent to attend;
- willingness to pay;
- verified commitment;
- unlocked demand;
- executed transactions.

Core business metric:

# VERIFIED DEMAND GMV

Supporting metrics:

- Demand GMV;
- Unlocked GMV;
- Executed GMV;
- Mara Revenue;
- effective take rate;
- join → commit conversion;
- unlock rate;
- Host response rate;
- repeat experience rate.

Do not sell identifiable user demand histories as a data product. Monetize transactions, performance, tools and aggregated demand intelligence.

---

## 3. MARKETPLACE ACTORS

### Demanders
People who propose or join experiences.

### Creators
People or characters who contribute audience, identity, IP, content, approval or presence.

### Creator Hosts
Authorized people or teams who operate experiences on behalf of a Creator.

### Venue Hosts
Businesses such as pubs, bars, restaurants, hotels, clubs, studios or event venues that can execute an experience.

### Operators
Independent organizers or production teams capable of executing an opportunity.

One entity may hold more than one role.

---

## 4. MARA ORCHESTRATES. HOSTS EXECUTE.

Mara should control the digital market infrastructure:

- discovery;
- demand aggregation;
- duplicate detection / clustering;
- commitments;
- payment orchestration when provider eligibility is proven;
- QR / access primitives;
- reputation;
- Host proposals;
- Creator approvals;
- rules and compliance;
- revenue splits;
- refunds / disputes;
- analytics.

Mara should avoid becoming the default physical producer.

Asset-light principle:

> **MARA ORCHESTRATES. HOSTS EXECUTE.**

---

## 5. EXPERIENCES ARE BROADER THAN EVENTS

An Experience can be:

- physical;
- digital;
- hybrid.

Examples include legal entertainment, social, cultural, hospitality, creator, educational, gaming, fitness, food, travel or digital formats.

The platform must not assume every experience belongs to Mara Vera or to an adult creator.

Architecture must support:

- Mara Vera;
- another Creator;
- a Host;
- a community;
- no Creator at all.

Physical adult-oriented experiences must not become a route to sexual services, coercion, unsafe conduct or illegal activity. Payment never changes consent, venue rules or legal boundaries.

---

## 6. DEMAND MUST CONCENTRATE, NOT FRAGMENT

Marketplace liquidity depends on aggregation.

If users independently propose near-identical ideas, Mara should suggest an existing demand before creating a new pool.

Required concepts:

- canonical demand;
- variants;
- city segmentation;
- similarity detection;
- merge candidates;
- `BRING THIS TO MY CITY`.

Do not merge meaningfully different experiences just to inflate demand.

---

## 7. MONETIZATION STACK

Mara should capture a fair share of value created across the demand lifecycle.

Priority layers:

1. transaction take rate;
2. commitment / reservation economics where legally and operationally valid;
3. Host performance fee or Host Pro tools;
4. Creator royalty / licensing administration;
5. VIP, upgrades and experience extras;
6. post-experience products;
7. Host SaaS / CRM / analytics;
8. aggregated Demand Intelligence;
9. capacity-matching economics for idle inventory;
10. later promoted opportunities where marketplace trust remains intact.

Do not optimize only headline take rate.

Optimize:

`VALUE CREATED × CONVERSION × REPEAT × CONTRIBUTION MARGIN × TRUST`

---

## 8. HOST VALUE PROPOSITION

Do not sell a pub, hotel or venue “advertising”.

Sell a validated opportunity.

Canonical Host proposition:

> **There are people near you who want this, and we can show the quality of that demand.**

A Host opportunity may expose aggregated metrics such as:

- city;
- category;
- interested users;
- verified commitments;
- average willingness to pay;
- estimated demand GMV;
- target capacity;
- preferred time window;
- Creator / IP requirements.

Never expose private individual buyer data as part of the opportunity listing.

---

## 9. CREATOR ECONOMY FITS INSIDE THE MODEL

The existing creator-economy thesis remains valuable because creators can originate or amplify demand.

Creators may:

- propose experiences;
- allow community-created experiences around their IP;
- approve Hosts;
- define royalties;
- define cities / formats / boundaries;
- attend physically where optional and supported;
- license a repeatable Experience Template without attending.

This can create an **Experience Franchise** layer without requiring a traditional franchise structure.

---

## 10. V1 PRODUCT BOUNDARY

Do not overbuild infrastructure before validating demand behavior.

V1 must prove:

1. users can discover public demand ideas;
2. users can join;
3. users can state willingness to pay;
4. users can propose an idea;
5. the product can suggest similar existing demand;
6. demand progress is visible;
7. `HOST WANTED` exists as a marketplace state;
8. Hosts can express synthetic interest in the prototype;
9. the system can calculate Demand GMV / Verified Demand GMV concepts;
10. the prototype remains isolated from production money and operations.

No production payments, payouts, database migrations, live Host contracts or physical fulfillment are authorized by this amendment.

---

## 11. OPERATING PRINCIPLE

Mara should remain operationally light for the founder.

The product should make users create demand and let third parties execute supply.

The founder should not become the dispatcher for every experience.

Highest-leverage outcome:

> **Mara knows what people want, how much real demand exists, and which trusted supply can fulfill it — then earns when that market clears.**
